> When the world still had heroes. When the greatest of them was a Halfling who could not say no. When the sky learned to smile.

## Gunnar Guirela and the Bow from the Sea

**~180 AV — Wind's Rest, East Harka**

On the eastern coast of Harka, in a small fishing village called **<a href="/oversee/locations/Wind%27s%20Rest">Wind's Rest</a>**, there lived an old man named **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gunnar%20Guirela">Gunnar Guirela</a>**.

He was a simple fisherman. Every day he went to sea, pulled his nets, and sold his catch at the market. He had no treasure, no magic, no ambition. He had only an old boat and a sagging hut by the shore.

One morning, when Gunnar pulled his net, he felt an unusual weight. Not a large fish — something long and heavy, tangled in the mesh. He pulled with difficulty, and when the object surfaced, he nearly fainted.

A **bow**.

A longbow with pale wood, almost transparent. Its curve was perfect. Its string was invisible — like air compressed into a line. The bow was neither wet nor dry, and it was **warm to the touch**.

Gunnar did not know that this bow was **<a href="/oversee/items/Gustaero%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Swan%20Bow">Gustaero</a>** — one of the six legendary weapons once collected by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Simon%20Greenbottle">Simon Greenbottle</a>**. He did not know that the bow had been thrown into the sea by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Firsa">Lei Firsa</a>**, the mother of **<a href="/Klan%20Lei">Klan Lei</a>**, to erase every trace of Simon from the world.

He only knew the bow was special. And he had to keep it safe.

Gunnar hid the bow in the attic of his hut, wrapped in old cloth. He never used it. He never told anyone — except his grandson **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf</a>**, who often came to help pull the nets.

> *"Grandfather, what bow is that?"* young Olaf asked one day, eyes wide.

> *"This is a family heirloom, child,"* Gunnar whispered. *"One day, you will understand. But remember — true strength is not in the weapon. It is in the heart that holds it."*

Olaf never forgot those words. He grew up. He learned to sail. He learned to shoot. And when his grandfather died, the bow was the only inheritance he received.

## Lei Alejandro and the Goblin Queen

**~19X AV**

Years later, when **Simon's** strength as the Valla began to fade, **six of the finest heroes in the world were gathered**.

They were led by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>** — a direct descendant of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Firsa">Lei Firsa</a>** from the **Lei Wendigo** line. He inherited unparalleled **pure wind magic** and **<a href="/oversee/items/Emelanica">Emelanica</a>** — the green time stone pulsing in his chest. His white hair and beard were immaculate, giving him an aura of wisdom. His green and gold robes symbolized the pride of his bloodline. But his calm eyes hid a wound that never healed.

At the height of his power, Alejandro made a journey to a remote region and, by accident, entered the territory of the **Goblin Kingdom**.

There, underground, in a place never touched by sunlight, he met **Queen <a href="/Ratu%20Ghaniba">Ratu Ghaniba</a>** — a wise and strong leader, utterly unlike the disgusting image long attached to her race. The long conversation in the goblin throne room became something Alejandro had never planned.

**He fell in love.**

The forbidden romance shook the **<a href="/oversee/factions/One%20Voice%20Commonwealth">Commonwealth</a>**. Nobles and wizards denounced him — calling him a race traitor, a disgusting man who defiled Simon's bloodline. Alejandro and Ghaniba married in a small ceremony, attended only by Ghaniba's most loyal followers and a few brave goblins. They hoped to build a new life on the border between two worlds.

The happiness was brief.

**Queen Ghaniba died in childbirth.** Their child was stillborn.

Alejandro returned home with a shattered heart and a dilemma: **he had no pure heir to inherit Emelanica.** In grief beyond measure, he made a decision that only those who have lost everything could understand. Following the ancestral tradition of the Lei, he entrusted Emelanica to the first descendant of Lei — the child who had died.

## Atlan Nouveau and the Woman from Another World

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atlan%20Nouveau">Atlan Nouveau</a>** was an ordinary human, born and raised on the **Rioquan** continent — in a coastal city always swept by sea winds.

His brown hair, always parted down the middle, his wary eyes, and his habit of carrying strange tools on his leather belt were his unchanging features since youth. He was not of noble blood. He was not the heir of any magical bloodline. Atlan was simply a young man with great curiosity and an unbounded love for the sea.

Since childhood, Atlan had spent more time at the docks than at home. He learned to read from old maps discarded by sailors, learned to write by copying the logbooks of ships at anchor, learned mathematics from counting tides and currents.

As a teenager, he worked as a dockworker, then as a navigator's assistant on a small merchant vessel — and finally, after saving a ship from a storm with his accurate current calculations, he was hired as a marine expert at the **<a href="/The%20Observatorium">Observatorium</a>** of **<a href="/Kota%20Juto">Juto Port City</a>**.

The Observatorium became his second home. A white stone tower on the edge of a cliff, with a basement that extended down to the shoreline, where he installed tide gauges, water thermometers, and current traps of his own design. At the top of the tower, he installed a great telescope — used not only to observe the stars, but to study the relationship between the moon's phases and the waves.

His colleagues at the Observatorium found him amusing but strange — a young man who could spend three days and three nights just recording wave patterns, then write poetry about the sea in the margins of his scientific notes.

In one corner of the Observatorium, Atlan kept a private collection: bottles of seawater from every corner of the world, each labeled with date, location, and notes on its color and taste. People laughed when they heard. Atlan did not care. The sea was his first love, and he did not need to explain love to anyone.

His quiet life changed one morning after a storm, when he found a **woman** washed up on the beach below the Observatorium.

She wore a torn purple robe, held a moon-shaped staff that still glowed faintly, and spoke in a language no one in Rioquan recognized. Atlan — used to communicating with sailors from many lands — tried every language he knew. None worked. But the woman's eyes spoke a language that needed no words. There was fear there. But also **resolve**.

Atlan brought her to the Observatorium and gave her dry clothes. For weeks, they communicated through gestures, drawings, and looks.

Slowly, the woman learned the Rioquan language. Her name was **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Reins">Reins</a>** — and she came from a place called **Aede**: another world connected to Oversee through a portal that could not be explained. She was a warrior destined to fight a terrible entity called **Raja Calamity Gonado** in her own world. But the portal had brought her here — to the beach below the Observatorium, into Atlan's life.

Atlan listened to Reins's story with wide eyes. As a scientist, he should have been skeptical. But how could he be skeptical of the woman standing before him — whose robe was still wet from the sea of another world, whose staff still glowed with a light no science could explain?

Reins knew she had to return. Gonado was waiting in Aede. But days became weeks, and weeks became months, and Reins was still there. Atlan never asked her to stay. He only showed her the beauty of this world — waves breaking on the reef, stars glittering in the sky of Rioquan, warm laughter of fishermen in the port tavern.

And one night, as they sat at the top of the Observatorium watching the full moon reflect on the sea, Reins made the decision to stay. Gonado could wait. Perhaps he would be defeated by someone else. Perhaps she was simply tired of fighting. All her life she had lived only for duty, for a destiny others had assigned her. But here, with Atlan, she felt like an ordinary person.

They married in a small ceremony on the beach, attended by fishermen and Atlan's colleagues from the Observatorium. Years later, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Mira%20Nouveau">Mira</a>** was born — a child with her father's brown eyes and her mother's mysterious smile.

Atlan built a swing beneath the Observatorium, taught Mira the names of stars and the patterns of waves, read her stories about the sea before sleep. For the first time in his life, he felt **whole**.

## Atreus Grin

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atreus%20Grin">Atreus Grin</a>** was a prince of the **<a href="/oversee/locations/Kerajaan%20Grin">Grin Kingdom</a>** — the land of those who carried the **eternal smile** that never faded.

Contrary to common misunderstanding, the Grin Kingdom was not inhabited by a single race with a particular skin color. Humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, and many other races lived side by side — united not by blood or lineage, but by the same curse: **the eternal smile carved into their faces from birth**.

The smile did not choose race. It did not choose skin color. It did not choose status. It was the inheritance of the tragic pact between **<a href="/Gavaevodata">Gavaevodata</a>** — the elf princess who dared to love a demon — and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Zargoth">Zargoth</a>** — the demon of the Gap who dared to love her back.

Atreus himself was a small-bodied elf with pale gray skin — a common color among Grin elves of the barren lands, but not all Grin had it. There were Grin with the brown skin of desert-human lineage, Grin with the pale white skin of mountain dwarves, even Grin with the greenish tint of half-orcs who had joined their kingdom. But one thing united them all: **a wide smile that never faded, no matter what they felt.**

Atreus chose to live close to nature — to be a druid who healed the land, because that was where he found a peace the outside world could not give him.

The Grin Kingdom was always viewed with suspicion. Their eternal smile was seen as a demon mark — proof of the Gap's blood flowing in their veins. And it was true — demon blood flowed there. But that did not make them evil.

Atreus watched his own people — humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, all races without exception — driven from markets. He watched Grin children stoned in the streets. He watched merchants raise prices tenfold the moment they saw the eternal smile. A human mother with an eternal smile was driven from her own birth village. A dwarf master craftsman could never sell his work in a human city because his face was always smiling. A halfling farmer was never allowed to sit in the same inn as other races.

The smile, to the outside world, was an **ineffaceable stigma**.

Atreus joined **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Initial%20Six">The Initial Six</a>** not for personal ambition. He wanted to prove that the cursed smile was not a mark of evil, but a symbol of resilience. He wanted to show that his people — who had survived the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Rapture">Rapture</a>** because they were deaf to **<a href="/oversee/lore/Ambitio-Ether">Ambitio-Ether</a>**, whatever race they were — were proof that the world needed **diversity, not uniformity**.

With his druidic power, he healed wounded earth, called roots to protect villages, replanted felled forests. Slowly, people began to see him not as a "smiling demon," but as a "friendly forest guardian." He carried the hope that one day the eternal smile would no longer be seen as a curse, but as a reminder that even from the forbidden union of elf and demon, resilience could be born — a resilience that could survive beyond ambition and ruin.

## Sam Hurls

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>** was a slightly chubby human man with wavy black hair and conspicuous clothing — purple and red with gold embroidery at every corner. His stringed instrument was never far from his grasp, and his fat fingers could pluck the most beautiful notes ever heard.

As a **bard**, Sam was a collector of stories. He had wandered farther than anyone — listening to whispers in port bars, old songs in remote villages, horrifying tales from travelers who survived the ends of the world.

He knew every story had two sides, and that the truth was often hidden between the notes that were never played.

His meeting with **<a href="/oversee/pcs">The Five Sticks</a>** — centuries after his death — would happen in **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>**, in a horrifying form. But in his own time, Sam was only a wandering minstrel caught up in the greatest adventure in history. He joined because he heard that this adventure would produce the best song he had ever written.

And he was right. He would write that song. But it would not be a song of victory — it would be a **quiet requiem** creeping from behind the stars.

## Olaf Guirela

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf Guirela</a>** was a green-skinned **half-orc** — a large, muscular body, a bald head, a thick gray beard, scars on his shoulders and arms. He wore simple clothing — rough brown cloth, a large red bead necklace, a faithful bow on his back.

Olaf was a follower of **iron discipline and simplicity**. He believed that true strength did not come from magic or magical weapons, but from **relentless training**. In his youth, he trained **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>** — later known as Gordon "The Noisy Rhino" — with brutal methods.

From that training, a philosophy was born that Gordon passed to **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Husgus">Husgus</a>**: **the true guardian is not the one who chases fame, but the one who protects the community.**

Olaf bequeathed two important things: **skepticism toward gods** (the seed of Husgus's atheism) and the **Gustaero bow** — the bow that Gordon would one day give to Husgus as a final inheritance. He taught that gods, if they existed, need not be worshipped — they need only be understood as part of the universe.

In The Initial Six, Olaf was the voice of reason. His old eyes had seen too much betrayal to trust sweet words. His skin crawled whenever he was near the ritual circle. But in the end, even his instincts were not enough to save them.

## Gzirmo Kopling

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gzirmo%20Kopling">Gzirmo Kopling</a>** was a **dragonborn** with orange-gold scales, small horns on his head, tribal tattoos on his face, and a posture impossible to ignore. His silver armor, with gear-accented shoulders, was a perfect fusion of technology and magic — an extension of his living, breathing body.

He came from a dragonborn clan in the highest mountains, near the lair of the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Three%20Surviving%20Dragons">ancient dragons</a>**. From his ancestors, he inherited extraordinary physical strength and a deep understanding of **mechanisms** — how wheels turn, how pressure works, how energy flows.

Among The Initial Six, Gzirmo was the quietest. He did not talk much about his past or the family he had left behind in **<a href="/oversee/locations/Elis">Elis</a>**. But in his sharp eyes lay the sorrow of a warrior who had seen too many battles and too little peace.

He joined because he believed in the mission: **to find a new Valla to save the world.** Nothing more, nothing less. His descendants would stay in Elis, scattered across the continent, waiting for the time to play their role in an unwritten history.

## Olaf and Sam in the Domido Forest

**~190 AV**

In the years before the **Great Disappearance** — when The Initial Six was still whole, and their names were beginning to be known across Oversee — **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf Guirela</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>** took a journey together.

Not for a great adventure. They wanted a moment of peace from the noise of the world.

They arrived at **<a href="/Hutan%20Domido">Domido Forest</a>** — a quiet region in the interior of **Harka**, far from the great cities and political intrigue. On the edge of the forest, near the border of two villages — **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>** to the north and **<a href="/oversee/locations/Gudameng%20Town">Gudameng</a>** to the south — they found a **rundown hut**.

Inside it, a **boy named <a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>** lived alone. He was about ten years old. His parents had died in a hunting accident years before.

Olaf, whose heart was moved by the sight of a small child living all alone, decided to stay a few weeks. Sam, with all his warmth, agreed. They had no special agenda — they just wanted to give the child a little happiness before returning to the world that was waiting.

Olaf taught Gordon how to **hunt**, **read animal tracks**, **set traps** — and most importantly, his philosophy of life: **true strength does not come from magic or magical weapons, but from relentless training, discipline that never slackens, and a clean heart.** He also taught Gordon to be **skeptical of gods and rulers**, because they often had their own interests — interests that were not always good for the common people.

Sam, on the other hand, taught Gordon about **lighter things**.

He taught him music. And a particular melody — notes that were cheerful yet slightly melancholic, which were said to be able to open doors between dimensions. Sam laughed as he taught it, saying that if Gordon ever found himself in trouble, he could sing the melody and maybe someone would hear — even Sam himself, from wherever he was.

Gordon did not understand what he meant. But he memorized the melody faithfully. He sang it every night before sleep, and sometimes, when the forest was quiet, he felt something moving among the trees — as if listening.

Before leaving, **Olaf gave Gordon something precious**.

From behind his cloak, he took out a **longbow wrapped in old cloth**. When the cloth was removed, Gordon saw a pale bow with a perfect curve that felt warm to the touch.

It was the **Gustaero bow**, Olaf said — **not for killing, but for protecting**. He had inherited it from his grandfather, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gunnar%20Guirela">Gunnar Guirela</a>**, a fisherman in Wind's Rest who had found the bow tangled in his nets one day and had kept it for years.

Now the bow was entrusted to Gordon.

Gordon accepted it with trembling hands, feeling he was still too small to use it. Olaf answered that he would grow, and the bow would wait for him — reminding him that this was not a weapon for killing enemies, but a tool for protecting the weak.

They left after a few weeks, leaving Gordon with a bow in his hand and a melody in his heart.

Gordon never saw them again. Some years later, he heard that **The Initial Six** had disappeared mysteriously in a ritual. But he did not know the details, and as a young boy, he could only mourn the loss of two people who had once been his teachers.

He kept the bow carefully. And sometimes he sang the melody Sam had taught him — especially when he felt lonely. He did not know that the melody would live on in the villages around him, sung by children who did not know its origin, and that centuries later, it would **call back Echo Sam Hurls from the darkness**.

## The Great Disappearance

**~200 AV**

The ritual could not use the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Gemhearts">Gemhearts</a>**.

**<a href="/oversee/items/Emelanica">Emelanica</a>** had been given to the Goblin Kingdom, following the dying wish of **<a href="/Ratu%20Ghaniba">Ratu Ghaniba</a>**. **<a href="/oversee/items/Safayer">Safayer</a>** was hidden in the belly of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Vythrax">Vythrax</a>** — the silver dragon poisoned by the stone. **<a href="/oversee/items/Rubion">Rubion</a>** had been given to the first descendant of **Lei Firage** in Rioquan. **<a href="/oversee/items/Topaju">Topaju</a>** — the yellow stone that anchored the world — had never been found since the last **Lei Roiga** held it.

Alejandro had only the remnants of knowledge from ancient **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Cycle%20Clan">Cycle Clan</a>** manuscripts and the pure wind magic flowing in his blood. But he was the strongest wizard of his generation, and he believed that was enough.

The **six heroes** gathered at the prepared altar.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>** led, in his green and gold robes, his white hair blown by the magical wind he himself had created.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atreus%20Grin">Atreus Grin</a>** stood beside him with the eternal smile that never faded, his small hands ready to summon the power of nature.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>** plucked the strings of his lute, creating a melody said to open doors between dimensions.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gzirmo%20Kopling">Gzirmo Kopling</a>** stood straight in his silver armor, the mechanical systems on his shoulders turning slowly.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atlan%20Nouveau">Atlan Nouveau</a>** — the ordinary human who loved the sea — brought his notes and measuring tools, ready to contribute his sharp mind.

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf Guirela</a>** — the old half-orc with the bow on his back — stood with an alertness that never faded, though age had long since claimed him.

They believed this ritual was meant to summon a new Valla — to find a soul willing to replace **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Simon%20Greenbottle">Simon</a>**. Lei Alejandro had explained that this was the only way to save the world from the hunger of the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Overseer%20%26%20The%20Architect">Overseer</a>**. No one doubted the words of a descendant of **<a href="/Klan%20Lei">Klan Lei</a>** — a respected hero, a man who had lost his wife and child in tragic circumstances.

**The ritual began.**

Lei Alejandro raised both hands. His eyes fixed on the vortex of energy beginning to form above the altar. His words echoed — calm, authoritative — instructing them to look at the vortex without fear.

Atreus felt a vibration beneath his feet. Roots in the earth moved, as if welcoming something.

Sam heard notes he did not want — not a fanfare of victory, but a **quiet requiem** creeping from behind the stars.

Gzirmo checked his armor indicators, confirming energy parameters were stable in the red zone, and asked Alejandro to verify the calculations for the vessel's capacity.

Atlan's mind drifted to **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Reins">Reins</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Mira%20Nouveau">Mira</a>** — a strange premonition that tomorrow's sun would feel colder than usual.

Olaf, skin crawling, warned the others that something was **wrong**. His instincts had never lied.

Alejandro spoke again — reassuring them not to fear pain, because pain was only a signal that they were trading something mortal for **absolute immortality**.

Atreus looked at the sky above the altar and saw something the others did not — **the sky was smiling**. Wide, like his own face. And he realized that the **Overseer** was very hungry — just like his ancestor once was.

Sam plucked his lute strings harder, intending to make the **Great Hunger** hear their music and come down to dance on the ashes.

Then — suddenly — **Gzirmo realized that his armor indicators were lighting up with a pattern he had never seen before.** There was an **external override** on his movement system. He could not step back.

**Atlan** felt the mana flow reverse — this was not channeling into a portal, this was **extraction**. Alejandro was **draining their souls**.

**Olaf** screamed a warning: the circle was not a shield — it was a **cattle pen**. He ordered the others to draw their weapons.

**Lei Alejandro lowered his head.** For the first time, his voice lost its authority. With a tone full of regret, he apologized — explaining that **a foundation that stands must be built from the hardest stone**, and that **only their souls were strong enough to hold back His hunger**.

**Atreus laughed for the last time.** His eternal smile widened — not from joy, but from bitter irony. He said that this smile, this curse, would **haunt Alejandro** — and let history record him as a monster, but **he would be the one who laughed last**.

**Sam** felt his veins sucked dry. His lute began to crack. He realized this sour note of betrayal was a bad climax — **Alejandro had killed the music right before the best part**.

**Gzirmo** watched the indicators on his armor go dark one by one — his system failing — and in the final seconds he realized that **Alejandro had not accounted for the variable of conscience**.

**Atlan**, with the last of his strength, screamed as loud as he could — hoping his voice would pierce the altar's barrier and reach across time to **Mira**. **This was not destiny. This was murder. And the truth must not die with them.**

**Olaf** — the old half-orc who had survived hundreds of battles — managed only final words before his body collapsed: he called Alejandro a **coward** who lacked the courage to face his own death, who **stole their breath to prolong his own life**.

Five souls shot upward, piercing the layers of reality, hurtling toward the presence of the **Overseer** — toward **Simon**, who sat as the **Valla**.

**Alejandro did not use Safayer to purify their souls.**

The purification was **imperfect**.

Their identities — **Atreus's** memories of the barren land of the Grin Kingdom, the eternal smile that was both curse and pride, the roots he summoned to protect small villages. **Sam's** grudge against the notes he never finished, the songs he wrote for village children, the melodies he should have passed to the next generation. **Atlan's** love for the sea, for Reins, for Mira. **Gzirmo's** loyalty to the warrior's code of honor, to the clan he left in Elis. **Olaf's** wisdom passed to Gordon, the philosophy of the guardian of balance he instilled in his student** — all of it was not fully erased.

They were sucked into Simon as **impure food** — creating something new inside the Valla's cracking soul. From that imperfection, **the <a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Echoes">Echoes</a> were born**.

**Lei Alejandro stood alone at the silent altar.**

The bodies of his five companions lay around him — lifeless, eyes open, staring at a stone ceiling they would never see again. The pure wind still swirled slowly around him, as if waiting for his next command.

But Alejandro did not move.

He only stood there, looking at his hands — the hands that had just killed his own friends — and asking himself whether **Ghaniba** would forgive him if she knew.

Before the Overseer, **Simon received a new surge of energy**. His soul, which had been weakening, was reinforced. But something strange happened.

Sometimes, in the gaps of his consciousness stretched between two worlds, he heard **foreign voices** — Atreus's laughter, Sam's lute, Gzirmo's mutterings about a failed system, Atlan's whispers about waves, and Olaf's heavy voice reminding him of someone he had never known.

Simon did not know that he now carried **five souls** inside him — not fully dead, not fully alive. Only **echoing**.

## The Legend

This event became known as **Divine Hunger II** — though the world more commonly called it **"The Great Disappearance."**

The official version spread among the people was that The Initial Six had performed a final ritual to communicate with the divine forces, and had vanished without a trace — perhaps ascending to godhood, perhaps sucked into another dimension, perhaps becoming part of the starry sky.

Beautiful speculations grew into legend, and their names were immortalized in statues across many cities.

But behind the scenes, the truth was kept tightly by the **<a href="/oversee/factions/Keeper%20of%20Secret">Keeper of Secret</a>** — an organization founded by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Mira%20Nouveau">Mira Nouveau</a>**, Atlan's daughter, who was still a teenager when she witnessed with her own eyes how **Lei Alejandro** used his power not to create, but to **erase**.

She and the other servants who were silent witnesses that night swore in silence that one day the truth would be revealed.

Meanwhile, the impact of this event spread widely.

The disappearance of The Initial Six created a **power vacuum** that led to increased crime and instability in various regions. Those who suffered most were the **Grin descendants**. Without **Atreus Grin** — the hero who had for so long been a symbol that the eternal smile was not a curse — society once again viewed them with suspicion and hatred.

The **Grin Kingdom** slowly collapsed. Its people scattered and were persecuted. From the ruins of destruction rose **<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>** — an organization determined to uncover the truth behind the disappearance of The Initial Six and restore the dignity of their people.

They found small clues — Atreus's letter, sent before his departure, the last words that never reached their destination:

> *"Don't look for who. Look for what has been lost. All keys reside in Emelanica."*

## The Echoes

The ritual at the ancient altar ended — but not as **Lei Alejandro** imagined.

Without **<a href="/oversee/items/Safayer">Safayer</a>** — the blue soul-purifying stone hidden in the belly of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Vythrax">Vythrax</a>** — the purification of the five companions' souls was **imperfect**.

Without **<a href="/oversee/items/Emelanica">Emelanica</a>**, which he had given to the Goblin Kingdom, without **<a href="/oversee/items/Rubion">Rubion</a>**, given to the first descendant of **Lei Firage** in Rioquan, without **<a href="/oversee/items/Topaju">Topaju</a>**, whose location was never known — Alejandro relied only on his pure wind magic and the remnants of knowledge from worn manuscripts.

He succeeded in sending the souls of **Atreus**, **Sam**, **Gzirmo**, **Atlan**, and **Olaf** to Simon. But he **failed to erase the traces of their humanity**.

Their identities were not fully lost.

They were sucked into Simon as impure food. But instead of becoming perfectly digested energy, they **created something new** inside the Valla's cracking soul. Something Alejandro did not want. Something he had not accounted for in his calculations.

From that imperfection, **the Echoes were born.**

## Trapped Echoes

**<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Echoes">Echoes</a>** are not souls. They are not merely memories. They are **echoes** — fragments of consciousness trapped inside **Simon**, waiting for a moment to speak again.

Sometimes, when Simon is half-conscious between two worlds, **Atreus's voice** is heard laughing softly, commenting on the irony of fate. Sometimes, **Sam's lute** plays softly in the recesses of Simon's consciousness, creating melodies he has never heard before. Sometimes, **Gzirmo's muttering** about a failed system is heard like an unfinished mantra. Sometimes, **Atlan's whispers** about waves and about **Mira** flow like water that never stops flowing. Sometimes, **Olaf's heavy voice** echoes, reminding Simon of someone he has never known.

But the Echoes do not always stay silent inside Simon.

Under certain conditions — when the mortal world brushes too close to the reality where Simon dwells — these echoes can **seep out**. Like fog creeping through the cracks of a door that does not close tightly.

They search for places that have resonance with their memories — places they once visited, people they once loved, or even just locations with enough emotional energy to attract them.

There, they **take form** — not fully real, not fully illusion. Something in between.
