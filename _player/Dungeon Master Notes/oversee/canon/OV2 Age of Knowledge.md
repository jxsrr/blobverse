> [!info] The Age That Remembered
> The survivors did not rebuild. They studied. For the first time, mortals tried to understand the fire that had burned them.

## Age of Knowledge

The era after **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Rapture">The Rapture</a>** was dark, but it was not empty.

The civilizations that survived the magical destruction began to **study** what had happened to their ancestors. They were determined not to repeat the same mistakes. For the first time in the history of Oversee, mortals did not merely *use* **<a href="/oversee/lore/Ambitio-Ether">Ambitio-Ether</a>** — which in this age was called simply **mana**, or *the source of magical power* — they tried to **understand** it.

Philosophers, wizards, and scholars of every race began documenting the properties of mana, the patterns of its fluctuation, and its relationship to the great disasters that had erased civilizations before them.

Underground libraries were built — far from the reach of those still blind to the danger of cosmic hunger.

This era became known as the **Age of Knowledge**: the age when wisdom began to replace ambition, and understanding became the first shield against destruction.

## The Discovery of the Giant Humanoid

**~10XX BV**

An ancient astrogeologist named **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Guanik%20Wiseman">Guanik Wiseman</a>** was observing the sky from the **<a href="/oversee/locations/Tarakita%20University%20of%20Heroism">Tarakita University of Heroism</a>** that in the past was the third observatorium — the site that would one day become the foundation of the **Rioquan** continent.

Wiseman documented the approach of a semi-circular cosmic object drifting through space at an anomalous speed: **500 kilometers per hour**. Too slow for a meteor. Too fast for a natural object. Something was **braking** it.

After intensive observation, the object was revealed to have the anatomy of a **giant humanoid**, over **20 kilometers tall**, with facial features that were a grotesque fusion of insect and sea life.

For **eight years**, the object drifted toward Oversee's orbit, creating an optical illusion in which its shadow appeared larger than the moon. It sparked a cult of worship that called it **The Giant Humanoid**.

But an early space probe expedition revealed a truth that shattered sanity.

When the probe recorded the **back** of the entity, it found a giant crack running from the nape of its neck to the base of its spine.

The object, 20 kilometers tall, was completely **hollow**.

It was not a living entity. It was a dead skin. A **shed shell** — the cast-off remains of something that had once worn it.


After that event, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Guanik%20Wiseman">Guanik Wiseman</a>** was never the same.

Every night, when he closed his eyes, he saw what he had seen through the probe's magical feed. He saw the **eyes** of that shell — empty, yet somehow staring directly into his soul. The nightmare came every night without fail, haunting every second of his sleep with the same image.

But over time, the dream began to change.


It explained that it had held back the cosmic hunger alone for a very long time. But its strength was fading. It could no longer hold on by itself. The shell seen in the sky was its **old skin**, shed when it sacrificed itself to become the first seal.

And now, it needed help.

- The eternal hunger of the **Overseer**
- The **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Gap">Gap</a>** that hides Ambitio-Ether from that hunger
- The mechanism of the seal that has kept Oversee safe




If the Gemhearts could be found and used correctly, the Valla could be replaced without causing destruction.

Wiseman woke from his dream with a new and terrible understanding. He was not merely a witness to the greatest secret in the universe. He was now the **only mortal** who knew how to save his world — or, if he failed, how to destroy it.

## The Founding of the Cycle Clan

**~1040 BV**

Realizing he could not bear this responsibility alone, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Guanik%20Wiseman">Guanik Wiseman</a>** began designing an organization.

It had to be **secret**, because knowledge of the Overseer and the mechanics of the seal could be easily misused by those who were not responsible. It had to be **neutral**, bound to no kingdom or race, because the safety of the whole world had to come before everything. And it had to be **ascetic**, because the temptation to use this knowledge for personal power would always exist.

So the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Cycle%20Clan">Cycle Clan</a>** was born.

They were not an evil group in the conventional sense. They were closer to monks or scholars bearing a terrifying cosmic responsibility. Their members were philosophers, historians, and wizards of many races, who had sworn an oath to put the survival of Oversee above everything — including their own personal morality.

They swore to keep this knowledge secret, to protect the Gemhearts from those who would misuse them, and to ensure the ritual of replacement could be performed safely when the time came.



It was not a god, even if its role might seem divine to mortals. It was only a guardian — part of the same world as them. All it wanted was for the small creatures of Oversee to live freely, to enjoy their lives without being shadowed by cosmic terror.

It considered them all part of itself. As children born from its sacrifice.

## The Excavation of the Gemhearts

**~104X BV**




The excavation took years, even decades. Each Gemheart was guarded by challenges: ancient riddles, magical traps, or simply locations that were impossible to reach.

But the Cycle Clan worked patiently, silently, without drawing the attention of the great kingdoms that might have wanted the Gemhearts' power for their own purposes.


But that is a story for a later age.

## The Ritual of Replacement

**~10XX BV**

With the Gemhearts successfully excavated and understood, the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Cycle%20Clan">Cycle Clan</a>** began preparing the most important and most terrible ritual in their history: the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Cycle%20Ritual">Ritual of Replacement</a>**.



A volunteer would stand at the center of a specially prepared altar. The Gemhearts — which in this age belonged to no individual, and were used only as ritual tools — would be placed in strategic positions to create a **Stasis Field**: a space of mana-vacuum in which no Ambitio-Ether could leak out.



The most important thing about the ritual was its **reverence**.

The Cycle Clan never considered the ritual cruel or inhuman. They performed it with deep sorrow and profound respect for the victims — usually volunteers from the oldest and wisest members of the cult, or condemned prisoners who agreed to this sacrifice as a form of atonement. They believed that with a measured and solemn approach, the suffering of the Valla could be minimized, and balance maintained without unnecessary cruelty.

For thousands of years, the ritual continued quietly and in secret.

One by one, volunteers gave their souls to become cosmic guardians. One by one, the old Valla were released with honor. Oversee kept turning, unaware that before the Overseer, beyond the reach of their reality, nameless heroes kept fighting against a hunger that never went out.

But this calm would not last forever. **Ambition, like the Overseer's hunger, never truly dies. It only waits for the right moment to return.**

## The Grin Kingdom

**~10XX BV**

In the barren lands of the central continent — amid the arid soil that no kingdom wanted — stood a civilization of **Elves** who had survived a devastating civil war centuries earlier.

They were survivors who had, by accident, passed through the cruelest natural selection their race had ever experienced. In that civil war, the magic-users died one by one — killed by each other, or consumed by a cosmic hunger they did not understand.

What remained were Elves who were naturally **deaf to Ambitio-Ether**: creatures who could not sense or use magic at all.

They became known as the **Grin Kingdom** — a name that would one day carry a double meaning: *grin* as a smile, and *Grin* as a reminder of the first ancestor who dared to step into a place no mortal had ever imagined.



At one hundred and twenty years old — still very young for an Elf — the restlessness of her ancestors was already planted in her soul. The Grin Kingdom, standing on barren land no one wanted, was only a small dot on the world map: poor, marginalized, and constantly an easy target for stronger neighbors rich in magic.

Her father, **Ozymandias Grin**, was a tired king. Every year, envoys from neighboring kingdoms arrived with mocking smiles and longer lists of tribute demands. Every decade, a new faction tried to invade their barren land — not because it was valuable, but because the Grin Kingdom was too weak to fight back.

The only reason the small kingdom still existed was its hard-to-reach geography and the tenacity of Grin soldiers who fought without magic — relying only on muscle and will.




### The Manuscript

The greatest discovery in the history of the Grin Kingdom happened by accident.



There were no instructions on how to open the door to the Gap, but there was a faint hint written there: **when the walls of the prison thin, when Ambitio-Ether surges somewhere, the crack will open for those who seek it.**


And one night, when a great battle between two neighboring kingdoms created a massive surge of Ambitio-Ether, the ripple of energy reached the barren land of the Grin Kingdom.


Without telling anyone, she stepped in.

### The Gap


In the distance, strange structures: towers of bone and alien metal, rivers of slow black liquid, and small glittering particles like diamond dust — crumbs of Ambitio-Ether that leaked from the Valla and fell into this place.

And there she met creatures with skin the color of red clay baked by the sun. Some had small horns. Some had large curling horns. Their eyes shone yellow in the darkness, and long tails moved slowly behind them.

They were the demon races: **tieflings, imps, and devils** — the native inhabitants of the Gap.


The largest of them stepped forward, with curling horns like a crown.





He was no ordinary demon. Among the inhabitants of the Gap, he was known as an **observer** — a creature more interested in understanding the outside world than in merely eating the crumbs of energy that fell. For thousands of years he had sat at the edge of the Gap, watching through small cracks, observing human and Elven civilizations rise and fall like the tide.




- The **Overseer**, the hunger that eats Ambitio-Ether
- The **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Rapture">Silent Rapture</a>** that destroys great magical civilizations
- The truth that the demons of the Gap are scavengers, living on leaked crumbs
- And that the demons have an interest in keeping the "cage" of the outside world intact — because if the Overseer eats everything, they will starve too





### The Return


She carried an **engagement**.



### The Wedding

The wedding was held in silence and secrecy.



With vows spoken by a Grin elder, two creatures from different worlds were joined in an unbreakable bond.

### The Children


They inherited their father's red skin, but in a subtler shade — red like clay, not bright red. Small horns grew on their heads; some were only small bumps, some began to curl. Their eyes shone yellow in the dark.

But there were two most striking traits of this lineage.


**Second: deafness to Ambitio-Ether.** Like their mother, these descendants cannot use magic. Not a drop of Ambitio-Ether answers their call. They are entirely non-magical beings — an anomaly in a world full of wonder.

But that is precisely where their strength lies: **they are undetectable by the cosmic hunger of the Overseer.** They are safe from the Silent Rapture that destroys magical civilizations.


### The War


The neighboring kingdoms reacted with a mixture of fear and fury. They were called race traitors, demon servants, cursed red creatures.

Attacks began to rain down on the Grin Kingdom. At first they were small, but then the neighboring kingdoms allied. The Grin forces, relying only on muscle and nerve, were overwhelmed by enemy wizards. Every day, more soldiers died. Every night, more villages burned.





The risk was obvious: once the enemies were destroyed, there was no guarantee that the hungry demons would return.

But there was no other choice. With Ozymandias's approval, the plan was carried out.

### The Night the Door Opened


Demon blood, thick and black, mixed with ordinary red Elf blood on the ground.

And in the sky above them, a **giant crack opened**.

Not a small crack like twenty-five years before. It was a gaping wound in the night sky, dozens of kilometers wide, stretching from the eastern horizon to the western horizon.

From it came no sound, no light — only a darkness blacker than black.

And from that darkness, **thousands of demons poured out.**

Tieflings. Imps. Behemoths with burning red skin. Winged fiends with enormous bat wings. Shadow demons that were only moving silhouettes.

They descended onto the battlefield like locusts, attacking the wizards of the coalition army, tearing apart their spells, devouring the Ambitio-Ether that radiated from terrified bodies.

In a single night, the coalition army was destroyed.

### The Legacy

When dawn came, the battlefield was silent. No wizard corpses remained — all had been devoured. What was left were scattered weapons and the footprints of demons beginning to return to the Gap.

But **the door of the Gap could not be fully closed.**

Every few generations, the crack would open slightly, and the hungrier demons would try to come out again. The descendants of Grin had to be ready to face it.

From that night onward, the legend of the **Grin family** was born — the Elven family with a smile that never fades.

They were called "Grin" not only because of the name of the kingdom, but because of the eternal smile fixed on their faces.

That smile is a reminder of their origin: of an Elven princess who dared to love a demon, of a demon who dared to love her back, and of the night when the door of the Gap opened and changed everything.

Centuries later, the Grin Kingdom became known as the **"Kingdom of War and Smiles"** — a civilization built on a tragic pact between their ancestors and demonic entities.

To gain unmatched power and endurance to win the ancient war, their forebears made that pact, and its consequences were inherited by all their descendants: an eternal smile carved into their faces.

The smile is a symbol full of paradox.

On one hand, it is a **blessing** — a physical reminder of the resilience and sacrifice of their ancestors, a giver of steel resolve to every Grin descendant.

On the other hand, it is a **burden** — a curse that torments them emotionally, hiding their sadness, anger, and pain. Worse still, the smile is hated by ordinary magical powers, making them magically alienated and often misunderstood as evil creatures.

But that is precisely where their survival lies. In a world constantly haunted by the **Silent Rapture** that destroys magical civilizations, the Grin descendants — with their eternal smile and their deafness to Ambitio-Ether — are creatures **invisible to the cosmic hunger of the Overseer**.

They are the true survivors — the ones who can endure in a world where the magical are wiped out.

And in times of crisis, when other kingdoms begin to forget their fear and think again of attack, **the door of the Gap will open slightly** — enough to remind them that behind the eternal smile of the Grin family, there is a darkness ready to be called.
