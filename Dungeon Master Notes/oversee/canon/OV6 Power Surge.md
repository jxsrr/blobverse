> [!info] The Age of the Long War
> When a hunter became a laughingstock. When a kingdom fell. When two brothers were separated by the sky itself.

## Gordon — The Hunter of Domido

**~240 AV — Hutan Domido**

Years passed. **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>** grew into a strong young man — a hunter of rare skill, known throughout the **<a href="/Hutan%20Domido">Domido Forest</a>** for his tracking, his patience, and his uncanny ability to read the land.

The **<a href="/oversee/items/Gustaero%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Swan%20Bow">Gustaero bow</a>** was always with him, though he rarely used it for hunting. He preferred a simple hunting bow. Gustaero was too special, too precious to use casually. He kept it wrapped in cloth, hidden in a hollow beneath the floorboards of his hut, and occasionally took it out just to hold it — to feel the warmth of the wood, to remember the old dwarf who had given it to him.

He traveled often between **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/locations/Gudameng%20Town">Gudameng</a>**, selling his catch at their markets. He knew the people of both villages well — the hunters, the farmers, the mothers selling cakes, the children running through the streets.

He knew they had no real quarrel with each other. They differed only in cuisine — **fried rice versus fried noodles** — and that was not a reason for hatred, let alone war.

## The Conspiracy

**~260 AV**

Then, in the year 260 AV, something changed.

Merchants from the great cities — **<a href="/Padre%20City">Padre City</a>**, **<a href="/oversee/locations/Javan%20Town">Javan Town</a>** — began appearing more frequently in both villages. They brought luxury goods, silk cloth, cheap jewelry — and weapons of a quality far too fine for remote villages.

They also brought **rumors**. Whispers of the neighboring village's hostile intentions. Whispers of plans to steal secret recipes. Whispers of insults that Gordon had never heard before.

Gordon was suspicious.

He watched the merchants speak with village elders in hushed tones, and he watched tensions rise afterward. He examined the new weapons — arrows with edges too sharp, swords with engravings too precise — and knew they were not made in either village. He saw the small crests hidden on the hilts: **the seal of the Commonwealth**.

One night, while hunting deep in the forest, Gordon found something strange.

Near the border of the two villages, close to the ruins of an old fortress from an ancient war, he saw a group of people gathered. They wore dark robes, and on their chests was the Commonwealth crest. A small campfire burned in the center of their circle.

Gordon approached slowly, hiding behind bushes.

He heard them speaking.

One of them — a man with a heavy voice — said that the **project at Gunung Rayu** was nearing completion. The engineers said the tunnel would break into the underground city within months. If successful, they would gain access to artifacts that had existed only in legend.

Another replied that the traffic of workers and materials was becoming harder to hide. Villagers were asking questions. Travelers had reported strange activity at the foot of the mountain.

Their leader answered that they needed a **distraction** — something large, something loud, something that would make everyone forget about Gunung Rayu.

> *"These two foolish villages have been suspicious of each other for generations. They just need a little push. A little heat on a fire that already burns. While they are busy killing each other, no one will notice what happens at the mountain."*

Gordon did not understand what "ancient artifacts" or "underground city" meant. But he understood the heart of the conversation: **they wanted to start a war between Ghuffghuff and Gudameng to cover up a secret project at Gunung Rayu.**

Without thinking, Gordon stood and shouted.

The conspirators were startled. Some drew weapons. Others prepared spells. Gordon, with the courage of a young man who did not understand danger, charged toward them. He had no plan — only anger.

But before he reached them, an arrow flew from the darkness and struck his thigh. He fell, blood pouring from the wound.

The conspirators approached. But their leader — the man with the heavy voice — raised his hand.

> *"Leave him alive. Let him talk. No one will believe him."*

They left Gordon there, with a wound in his leg and a fire in his chest.

## Exile

**~260 AV**

The next day, limping and determined, Gordon went to the elders of **Ghuffghuff**.

He told them what he had seen. He told them about the Commonwealth agents, about the secret project at **<a href="/Gunung%20Rayu">Gunung Rayu</a>**, about the conspiracy to start a war as a distraction.

The elder of Ghuffghuff — an old man with a white beard and weary eyes — looked at him with something between pity and irritation.

> *"You are a hunter, Gordon. Not a politician. Gunung Rayu is a haunted place. Nothing there but rocks and fog. Perhaps you saw wrong. Perhaps you ate too many wild mushrooms."*

Gordon insisted. He asked them to send men to the location. The elder agreed — perhaps only to placate him.

But when they arrived at the site, there was nothing. The campfire was cold. The tracks were erased. The only thing remaining was Gordon's own blood on the ground.

People began to whisper. Perhaps he had fallen. Perhaps he had hit his head. Perhaps he was simply **mad**.

Gordon went home, devastated. But he did not give up.

He began his own investigation. He tracked the conspirators. He discovered that they appeared often in both villages, disguised as merchants. He gathered evidence — weapons bearing the Commonwealth crest, abandoned letters, even testimony from villagers who had seen strange things near **Gunung Rayu**.

Weeks later, tensions peaked.

A merchant from **Padre City** — one of those who had appeared frequently in both villages — was found dead at the border, near the **Wok Gate** of Ghuffghuff.

Ghuffghuff blamed Gudameng. Gudameng blamed Ghuffghuff. The fire began to burn.

And Gordon — who happened to be hunting near the site that night — became the **prime suspect**.

People said he often raged. He always spoke nonsense about a conspiracy at Gunung Rayu. Perhaps he had done it himself, to prove his theory.

Gordon had no alibi. He had been alone in the forest, as usual.

He was arrested, interrogated, and nearly lynched by a mob. But one wiser elder — perhaps one of the few who still remembered that Gordon had been a good orphan boy — defended him.

> *"Exile him. Drive him out. Let him live in the forest. That is more merciful than killing him."*

Gordon was **exiled**.

He returned to his hut in **Hutan Domido**, and from a distance, he watched the fires of war begin to burn.

The two villages that had once been peaceful now killed each other. The young men he had taught to hunt died on the battlefield. The mothers who had given him food wept for their dead children.

And everyone thought he was **mad**.

> *"Badak Berisik,"* they called him — **The Noisy Rhino**.

First as a joke. Then as a name that stuck.

Because he kept making noise that no one wanted to hear. Because he kept shouting even when no one listened.

## The Gruffgud War

The **Gruffgud War** lasted for centuries.

For the people of the great cities of **<a href="/oversee/locations/Harka">Harka</a>** — **<a href="/oversee/locations/Yawning%20Town">Yawning Town</a>**, **<a href="/Padre%20City">Padre City</a>**, **<a href="/oversee/locations/Javan%20Town">Javan Town</a>** — this war was a joke.

Merchants passing through the region brought stories of two foolish villages fighting over food. Bards composed mocking songs about the *"Rice vs. Noodle War"* and sang them in port taverns. People laughed when they heard that the two villages were fighting over **fried rice**, joking that fried noodles were also delicious and perhaps they should fight too.

Nobles in **Padre City** even held themed parties where guests dressed as cooks and pretended to battle with giant spoons and forks.

The joke was entertaining. No one thought to ask questions.

But behind the laughter, there was also a vague anxiety.

Some merchants who dared to pass through the conflict zone never returned. Some travelers reported strange things at **Gunung Rayu** — strange lights at night, sounds like explosions from inside the mountain, robed figures moving among the rocks.

But those reports were always dismissed by Commonwealth officials with sweet smiles, saying it was just two crazy villages killing each other and there was nothing to worry about.

Other cities in Harka — **<a href="/oversee/locations/Nesia%20Town">Nesia Town</a>** with its wheat fields, **<a href="/oversee/locations/Muangching%20Bay">Muangching Bay</a>** with its fishing harbor — heard only occasional news of the war. For them, Ghuffghuff and Gudameng were small dots on the map, too far to care about.

When refugees occasionally came with stories of the horrors of war, they were given a little food and sent away. The world kept turning, and the absurd war kept burning without anyone truly caring.

Meanwhile, in **Hutan Domido**, **Gordon aged**.

His age somehow moved slowly — perhaps because of the **Gustaero bow**, perhaps because of a curse, perhaps because he was **too stubborn to die**.

His hair began to whiten. His beard grew thick. His back began to bend. But his eyes remained sharp, and his voice still rumbled like a rhino.

He never stopped trying.

Again and again, he descended from the mountain and entered both villages. He spoke to elders, to warriors, to mothers who had lost children. He showed them the evidence he had collected — weapons bearing the Commonwealth crest, old letters, testimonies gathered over years.

But always, the evidence disappeared mysteriously. Witnesses died or vanished. And people continued to laugh at him.

They said the **Noisy Rhino** was still going on about his nonsense about **Gunung Rayu**. They thought he was old and perhaps **demented**.

Gordon did not care. He kept shouting, because he knew the truth — and he would not let that truth die with him.

## The Fall of the Grin Kingdom

**~270 AV**

Decades after **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Great%20Disappearance">The Great Disappearance</a>**, as the name **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atreus%20Grin">Atreus Grin</a>** began to fade from public memory — just as he had faded from the world — the **<a href="/oversee/locations/Kerajaan%20Grin">Grin Kingdom</a>** slowly collapsed.

Without its charismatic leader — without the figure who had been living proof that the eternal smile was not a curse — the Grin people faced the same bitter reality again.

The wider world, which for a few decades had begun to see Atreus as a hero, once again viewed them with suspicion and hatred. The eternal smile on the face of every Grin descendant — human, elf, dwarf, halfling, all races without exception — was once again considered a demonic stigma, proof of the Gap's blood flowing in their veins.

The persecution came in waves.

Grin merchants were driven from city markets. Grin children were stoned in the streets. Grin families who had settled in various regions were forcibly evicted from their homes. The Grin Kingdom — which had once stood as a symbol of resilience on the barren land no one wanted — began losing its citizens one by one.

Some went into seclusion in remote places. Some chose to hide their identities, covering their smiles with veils and masks. And some — the most stubborn, the most loyal to the legacy of **<a href="/Gavaevodata">Gavaevodata</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Zargoth">Zargoth</a>** — chose to **stay and fight**.

From the ashes of despair, **<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>** was born.

## The Will of Grin

The organization was founded by <a href="/oversee/npcs/Hildag%20Grin">Hildag Grin</a> — the Weaver, the former Wakil Pertahanan of the One Voice Commonwealth, the woman who had defected after the Initial Six disappeared. She had been building the organization for centuries, in secret, waiting for the right moment.

Hildag's daughter-in-law — <a href="/oversee/npcs/Geneva%20Grin">Geneva Grin</a> — and her son — <a href="/oversee/npcs/Accarus%20Grin">Accarus</a> — became the Smiling Hand, the co-leaders of field operations. Geneva was not a great warrior. She was not a charismatic orator. But she had unwavering conviction and a vision far beyond her time. Accarus was the muscle. Geneva was the brain. Together, they executed Hildag's design.

Not to attack the world that hated them — that would only justify the stigma. Not to hide and hope to be forgotten — that was the same as surrender. Their goal was greater than revenge or self-protection.

**<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>** stood on two main goals.

**The first goal was social acceptance.** They wanted to continue Atreus's vision — to show the world that the cursed smile was not a mark of evil. That Grin descendants — whatever race they were, whatever skin color they had, whatever their background — were ordinary humans, elves, dwarves, halflings who just wanted to live in peace like everyone else. They wanted to prove that the Gap's blood in their veins did not make them monsters, just as the demon blood of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gavaevodata%20Grin">Gavaevodata Grin</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Zargoth">Zargoth</a>** did not make their ancestors evil — only different, only marginalized, only surviving in their own way.

**The second goal was to find the truth.** They wanted to uncover the reason behind Atreus's sudden disappearance. What really happened at **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Great%20Disappearance">The Great Disappearance</a>**? Why did the six greatest heroes of their age vanish without a trace? And most importantly — why did Atreus, the only hero of the marginalized, **never return**?

But behind the two goals announced to members, a deeper motivation was hidden — known only to a few at the top of the leadership.

**The secret goal of Will of Grin** — the most crucial and most closely guarded — was to **collect the four Emelanica stones**.

This motivation came from Atreus's final message, found by Geneva among his belongings. The message was short, written hastily on a scrap of worn leather, but its words echoed through the centuries:

> *"All keys reside in Emelanica."*

No further explanation. No hint of what it meant. Only **six words** that became the obsession of the entire organization.

The members of Will of Grin interpreted this message in different ways, depending on who was asked and how deeply they were involved in the organization's secrets.

For most members, **Emelanica was the Answer Key**. They believed that the green stone, with its absolute knowledge, could reveal what had really happened to **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Initial%20Six">The Initial Six</a>**. With Emelanica, they could see the past — witness with their own eyes how Atreus disappeared — and find the truth long hidden. With that truth, they could clear Atreus's name and — by extension — clear the name of the entire Grin bloodline.

For others — especially the older ones more deeply involved in Grin mysticism — **Emelanica was the Key of Liberation**. There was an ancient belief, passed down through generations, that the combined power of the four stones — **<a href="/oversee/items/Emelanica">Emelanica</a>**, **<a href="/oversee/items/Safayer">Safayer</a>**, **<a href="/oversee/items/Rubion">Rubion</a>**, **<a href="/oversee/items/Topaju">Topaju</a>** — might **break the eternal smile curse** that had bound them for centuries. If the curse was the inheritance of Gavaevodata's pact with the Gap, then perhaps with the same ancient power, the curse could be undone. Grin descendants could live without the eternal smile, free from the stigma carried from birth.

But among the highest leaders, there was deeper speculation. Some of them — including **Geneva** and **Accarus** — began to realize that Atreus's message might not refer only to the stone itself. *"Emelanica"* might also refer to **its holder** — to the bloodline of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>**, to the still-living child of **<a href="/Ratu%20Ghaniba">Ratu Ghaniba</a>**, to something they did not yet fully understand. Perhaps the key was not just collecting the stone, but **finding the right person to use it**.

**<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>** was easy to recognize — not by uniform or emblem, but by the **cursed smile** that never faded from their faces. In a world that hated them for that smile, they made it their identity, their badge among peers. The eternal smile was their uniform, their flag, their proof that they were part of something greater than themselves.

They called themselves **Pirizia** — a group of fighters fiercely solid, loyal, and fanatical about the truth and honor of their tribe. Their solidarity was so strong that to outsiders they seemed an indivisible whole. They would only truly open up and trust fellow Grin descendants, because only they could understand the burden they carried. The outside world often saw them as cold, closed, full of suspicion — and that was true. Years of persecution had taught them that **trust is a luxury that must be paid for dearly**.

Their organizational structure was designed for efficiency and secrecy.

At the top of the hierarchy sat <a href="/oversee/npcs/Hildag%20Grin">The Weaver</a> — a position held by Hildag Grin, the founder of Will of Grin. She was not Geneva's mother. She was Accarus's mother — and Antrem's. She had hidden them from the Klan Lei. She had protected them. She had bred them. She was the Weaver. She was an old woman with a wrinkled but wide eternal smile, and eyes that had seen more history than anyone. Her nickname, **The Weaver**, was not without reason. Hildag was a master strategist who designed the fabric of Will of Grin's operations across the continent, like a weaver arranging threads into whole cloth. She never went into the field herself, but every movement of members in the field was part of the grand pattern she designed.

Below her was **The Smiling Hand** — an executive position held by **Accarus** and **Geneva** themselves. The two were responsible for operations across the continent, leading important missions directly, and making tactical decisions in the field. Accarus — with grief for Atreus that never healed — was the organization's muscle: a tough fighter who never retreated from battle. Geneva was its brain: a visionary who saw the big picture in the midst of chaos.

At the bottom layer stood the **Grinners** — the field troops who were the backbone of the organization. They were faceless soldiers, without names, without individuality. A Grinner had no personal ambition, no desire beyond the mission. They were trained to obey, to move as one unit, to sacrifice themselves without hesitation if necessary. To the outside world, they were just terrifying smiles emerging from the shadows, carrying out tasks, then vanishing again. But inside the organization, they were **family** — a large family united by the same curse, the same persecution, the same determination to change the world.

In their underground headquarters in **<a href="/oversee/locations/Maw%20Desert">Maw Desert</a>**, Pirizia, **<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>** continued to work in silence. They gathered information, tracked the trail of Emelanica, kept the secret of what really happened to **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Initial%20Six">The Initial Six</a>**.

They knew **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>** was still alive somewhere. They knew **<a href="/Klan%20Lei">Klan Lei</a>** was still in power. They knew the truth of the betrayal might be more terrible than they imagined.

But they were not afraid. They were the descendants of **<a href="/Gavaevodata">Gavaevodata</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Zargoth">Zargoth</a>** — born from the forbidden union of elf and demon, haunted by a smile that never faded, yet still enduring beyond ambition and destruction.

And in the heart of every member of **<a href="/oversee/factions/Will%20of%20Grin">Will of Grin</a>**, the same belief was kept: that one day, they would find **Emelanica**. That one day, the truth would be revealed. That one day, the eternal smile would no longer be a curse, but a **symbol of victory** over a world that had judged them for so long.

## The Keeper of Secret

**<a href="/oversee/factions/Keeper%20of%20Secret">Keeper of Secret</a>** was a secret organization in Oversee, dedicated to finding the truth about the disappearance of **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Initial%20Six">The Initial Six</a>**.

The organization was officially founded by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Mira%20Nouveau">Mira Nouveau</a>** — daughter of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atlan%20Nouveau">Atlan Nouveau</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Reins">Reins</a>** — who was the key witness to the entire incident.

At the time of the event, Mira was likely still a teenager. She witnessed with her own eyes how **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>** used the power of **<a href="/oversee/items/Emelanica">Emelanica</a>** — the pure elemental stone — not to create, but to **erase**.

The main and core mission of this organization was to preserve and reveal the true fate of Mira's father, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atlan%20Nouveau">Atlan</a>**.

## The Gruffgud War Ends

**~650 AV**

In the year 650 AV, after nearly four centuries of bloodshed, the two villages finally ran out of strength.

A new generation had been born and died without ever knowing what life without war felt like. The elders who remained were tired old people who no longer remembered why they were fighting. All they knew was the hatred passed from father to son, from mother to daughter, like a hereditary disease.

**Gordon** — now over four hundred years old, somehow still alive — descended from the mountain one final time.

His body was ancient. His hair was completely white. His beard hung to his chest. But his eyes still burned. And his voice still rumbled like thunder.

He gathered the elders of both villages at the old fortress in the middle of **<a href="/Hutan%20Domido">Hutan Domido</a>** — the same fortress where he had once seen the conspirators, where he had been wounded, where he had started all this. The fortress was weathered, its wood eaten by termites, its roof full of holes — but it still stood as a silent witness to history.

Gordon stood in the center of the room. The elders sat around him — some with skeptical expressions, some with curiosity, some with hope that had almost burned out.

> *"Look at yourselves,"* Gordon said, his voice shaking the old walls. *"Look at your hands. Are those the hands of killers? Or the hands of farmers, the hands of mothers, the hands of fathers who should be holding their children?"*

He opened his worn shirt, revealing the scar on his thigh — the same wound from four centuries ago, one that had never truly healed.

> *"This is the first wound,"* he said. *"Not from an enemy's arrow, but from the arrows of those who wanted you to fight. I saw them, that night. I heard their plan. They are digging something at **Gunung Rayu** — something they do not want anyone to know about. And they are **using you as puppets**."*

The elders were silent. Some began to weep.

> *"I have no more evidence,"* Gordon said. *"My evidence is gone. Burned. Erased. But I have **memory**. I have **eyes that saw**. And I have a **voice that will never be silent**. You can believe me or not. But know this: **this war is not your war**. You are puppets. And it is time for the puppets to cut their strings."*

The meeting lasted three days and three nights.

No one recorded it. No one wrote its history.

But in the end, the elders signed a peace treaty. **The Gruffgud War ended.**

Gordon did not stay to celebrate. He returned to his hut in **Hutan Domido**, alone.

He knew this peace was fragile. He knew the real enemy — **the Commonwealth** — was still out there, watching.

But at least, for now, **no more blood was spilled**.

## The Great Strike

**~674 AV**

On a bright afternoon, while fishermen were at sea and farmers were harvesting wheat in their fields, **something enormous fell from the sky**.

Some witnesses later swore it was a **meteor**. Others said it was a **fragment of an angry star**.

But whatever its name, the impact was the same: a disaster that changed the face of **Rioquan** forever.

A massive earthquake shook the ground. The sea raged, creating waves dozens of meters high that swallowed the coastline. Giant cracks split the continent, separating land that had been united for thousands of years into **two parts**: **Rioquan East** and **Rioquan South**.

In the center — at the epicenter of the disaster — thousands of souls vanished in an instant. Swallowed by the earth. Swept away by the sea. Crushed by the debris falling from the sky.

Among those at the very center of the catastrophe were a pair of young twin brothers — barely twenty years old, with their whole lives ahead of them. Their names were **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Accarus%20Grin">Accarus</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Antrem%20Klaus">Antrem</a>** — descendants of the Lei Firage line, hidden from the Klan by their mother, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Hildag%20Grin">Hildag</a>**. They lived with a foster family in Rioquan, far from the politics of the Grin Clan.

But unlike their cousins who lived in the grand palaces of **West Harka**, these two young men had not inherited the full power of the fire element. Their blood had been mixed too far, too diluted, too distant from the source of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Simon%20Greenbottle">Simon's</a>** power sitting as the **Valla**.

They could feel the warmth of fire at their fingertips. They could light a candle without a match. They could make a campfire burn brighter. But **nothing more**.

To Klan Lei, which glorified purity of blood, they were **failures** — accidental traitors to the lineage they were supposed to be proud of.

The two brothers lived in a small town in the central region of Rioquan — far from the bustle of the capital, far from the political intrigues of Klan Lei. They lived with a foster family — kind people who did not know their true bloodline — in a simple but sufficient life.

**Accarus** — a few minutes older — was a clever and restless young man, always wondering about the world outside, always feeling that something greater was waiting for him.

**Antrem** — his brother — was straighter and simpler. In Accarus's words, *"more foolish"* — a young man content with life as it was, who would rather spend his time helping neighbors tend livestock than think about great things.

On the day the sky fell, they were **not together**.

**Accarus was in the cellar** — the foster family's root cellar, where they stored grain for the winter. He had gone down to fetch a sack of flour for dinner. The stone walls were thick. The earth was cool.

**Antrem was in the fields** — helping the foster father harvest the last of the wheat before the autumn rains.

Their foster mother was in the kitchen, preparing dinner.

The sky suddenly turned red, then orange, then blinding white.

The roar was so enormous that they heard nothing afterward — only the vibration that rippled through the earth, through their bones, through their entire being.

## The Twin Who Stayed

When **Accarus** opened his eyes, he was in darkness.

He could not move. He could not breathe. He was buried — pinned beneath something heavy. His ribs ached. His head throbbed. He tasted blood.

He did not know how long he lay there. Perhaps hours. Perhaps days. He drifted in and out of consciousness. He heard voices — not human voices, just the groan of the earth settling, the hiss of dust, the distant roar of water.

When he finally crawled out, the world was **broken**.

The foster family's house was gone. The fields were gone. The road was gone. The world was a wasteland of cracked earth and shattered stone.

He called Antrem's name. He called his foster parents' names. No one answered.

He **searched for weeks**. He walked to refugee camps. He asked everyone he met. He showed Antrem's features to anyone who would listen. But no one had ever seen a young man with a face like his.

He believed **Antrem was dead**. He believed **everyone was dead**.

## Hildag

**Weeks later** — when the chaos had begun to settle, when the refugees had begun to move on, when the world had begun to forget — **Hildag Grin** came to Rioquan.

She had felt the Great Strike from Pirizia. She had felt the world break. She had felt the **fire in her blood** — the fire of her twin sons — flicker and dim.

She came to find them. She came to find **both** of them.

She found **one**.

She found **Accarus** — sitting in the ruins of the foster family's house, staring at the empty fields, waiting for a brother who was never coming home.

She did not tell him she was his mother. She did not tell him she had been searching for him for years. She simply said: *"You are not alone. Come with me."*

He did not know who she was. He did not know she had bred him. He did not know she had hidden him from the Klan. He only knew she was **the first person who had spoken to him in weeks** — the first person who had offered him a hand.

He took it. He followed her.

He never looked back.

## The Will of Grin

At the **Will of Grin** headquarters in **<a href="/oversee/locations/Maw%20Desert">Maw Desert</a>**, Pirizia, Accarus stayed and healed.

He was surrounded by people with the same smile — humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, all races with one thing in common: **an eternal smile that never faded**.

For the first time in his life, he did not feel **foreign**.

They did not care about his diluted Lei blood. They did not care about the weak fire he had inherited. They only saw him as someone who had survived a disaster — someone who needed shelter.

He trained there. He fought there. He became a soldier of the cause.

And there he met **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Geneva%20Grin">Geneva</a>** — a Grin woman from a smaller, less influential family. She was a soldier too. She was a survivor too.

They trained together. They fought together. They fell in love slowly, like a fire kindling from small embers.

Geneva saw in the young man's eyes a **steadfastness** she had been looking for — not the steadfastness of a warrior, but the steadfastness of someone who had lost everything yet still endured.

Accarus saw in Geneva's smile something he had never seen before: **acceptance without conditions**.

## Hildag's Revelation

For years, Accarus thought Hildag was just the leader of Will of Grin — a woman who had taken him in out of kindness. He did not know she was his mother. He did not know she had bred him. He did not know she had hidden him from the Klan.

**She told him the truth** — eventually. After Jarko was born. After he had proven himself. She told him: *"I am your mother. I hid you from the Klan. I hid you from the world. I am sorry."*

He forgave her. He always forgives her. She is his mother. She is the only family he has left — besides Antrem, who he believes is dead.

## The Wedding

They married a year later, in a small ceremony attended only by members of **Will of Grin**.

**Hildag** — Accarus's mother — was there. She smiled. She always smiles.

And on the night of the wedding, Hildag called Accarus to her private room.

She opened a long, cloth-wrapped bundle. Inside, pale and warm to the touch, lay a **bow**.

**<a href="/oversee/items/Gustaero%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Swan%20Bow">Gustaero</a>**.

Hildag explained that this bow was one of the **three ancient relics** — the neck of the swan. It had been carried by **Gunnar Guirela**, then **Olaf Guirela**, then **Gordon**. It had been waiting for someone who understood that it was not a weapon of war — it was a tool of passage.

She told him: *"This is not a weapon. It is a promise. Use it to protect our people."*

Accarus accepted **Gustaero** with trembling hands. He carried it for decades. He never used it to kill. He used it to protect.

**Accarus never carried Rubion.** Rubion was taken by the party from Jarko in Session 01, and later given to Mira. Accarus has never held it. He does not know where it is.

## The Twin Who Was Lost

Meanwhile, in Rioquan, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Antrem%20Klaus">Antrem</a>** survived in a different way.

When the giant wave struck, he was in the fields — far from the house, far from the cellar, far from his brother. The ground opened beneath him. He fell. He was swept away by the wave — carried out to sea, then washed ashore on the eastern coast of Rioquan, which had not been as devastated by the disaster.

He washed up in a small fishing village, with a bruised body and blurry memories. The fishermen cared for him, gave him food, gave him shelter.

When he had recovered enough, he began searching for his brother.

He walked to refugee camps, asked everyone he met, showed Accarus's features to anyone who would listen. But no one had ever seen a young man with a face like his.

Months passed. **Antrem finally stopped searching** — not because he had forgotten Accarus, but because **he had to live**.

With the remnants of money he earned from odd jobs, he bought a few sheep and started a new life as a **shepherd** on the slopes of the newly formed **South Rioquan**.

He never again spoke of **Klan Lei**. Of the fire he should have inherited. Of his twin brother lost at sea.

He simply became **Antrem** — a simple young man who worked hard from morning to night, who preferred the company of sheep to people, who never smiled too broadly because his smile was not eternal like the Grin — just ordinary.

## Antrem and Vreina

Years later, fate brought him to **Elis**.

A sheep merchant he knew offered him work on the other continent. Antrem — who had no ties left in Rioquan — accepted.

In Elis, he met **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Vreina%20Arleschia">Vreina Arleschia</a>** — a woman from a minor noble family who was not particularly ambitious.

Vreina was drawn to this quiet young man — not for his wealth or his looks, but for his **simplicity**. In her eyes, Antrem was unlike other men who only wanted to raise their status by marrying a noble. He only wanted to live quietly, tend sheep, and perhaps one day have a family.

They married in a small ceremony attended only by Vreina's family and a few of Antrem's neighbors. Antrem knew nothing about dowries or noble traditions — he came with the best clothes he had, a bunch of wildflowers, and a **sheep** as a gift.

Vreina's family — already tired of noble pretense — liked this sincerity.

Years later, **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Vya%20Arleschia">Vya</a>** was born — their daughter, a child with distinctive **pink hair**, sharp eyes, and a smile that never faded even though she was not a Grin descendant.

Or perhaps, between the blood of a simple shepherd and the blood of a minor noble, **something older had awakened** — something connected to the **Lei Firage** bloodline, to the fire that no longer burned in Antrem, to a destiny not yet revealed.

Antrem knew nothing about that. He only knew he loved his daughter more than anything in the world.

He taught Vya how to feed sheep, how to milk them, how to make cheese. He never told her about his past — about the twin brother lost at sea, about the weak fire in his veins.

For him, the past had died with his parents on the day the sky fell. All that existed was **now** — the green fields of Elis, the faithful sheep, the wife he loved, and the little daughter who made him smile every day.

## Antrem's Departure

**~682 AV — Chenalia**

That year, **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Vya%20Arleschia">Vya</a>** was only **four years old**.

**<a href="/Chenalia">Chenalia</a>** was as usual — quiet, green, surrounded by gently rolling hills like frozen waves. Vya spent her days chasing butterflies in the meadows, or sitting on her mother **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Vreina%20Arleschia">Vreina's</a>** lap, listening to stories about stars and seas she had never seen.

Her father, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Antrem%20Klaus">Antrem</a>**, was a quiet man. Vya was used to seeing him sit on the veranda, staring at the sky with empty eyes, as if searching for something no one else could see. Sometimes, at night, she heard him mutter in his sleep — unfamiliar names, words in a strange language.

One afternoon, **a stranger came to the village**.

Vya was playing by the river when she saw him: a tall man with long black hair, sharp eyes, and the strangest thing — a **smile that never faded** from his face. Not a happy smile, but a smile **fixed permanently**, like a mask that could not be removed.

At his temples grew small curling horns.

Vya hid behind a bush, watching from a distance as the man walked toward their house. She heard her mother's startled voice, then long whispers she could not understand.

That night, her father sat in Vya's room longer than usual, stroking her hair with a slightly trembling hand.

Slowly, he told her that he had to go away for a while — for something important — but he would return, he promised.

Vya asked where. Her father answered: to a far place, with the man who had come — a man named **Accarus**, who was called his **brother**.

Vya did not know her father had a brother. Antrem had never spoken of the past.

But she saw something in her father's eyes — not a lie, but a deep sadness.

When she asked when he would return, **Antrem smiled** — a forced smile, different from the stranger's — and said *soon*, with a promise.

The next morning, **Antrem left**.

Vya stood at the doorway, waving her small hand, watching the two figures disappear behind the hill.

**Vreina** stood behind her, silent, her hand gripping the edge of her dress.

She did not know that this was the last time she would see her husband.

## Vreina's Conspiracy

**~683 AV**

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Vreina%20Arleschia">Vreina Arleschia</a>** was not a foolish woman.

She knew who had come for her husband — **Accarus**, leader of **Will of Grin**, a secret organization fighting for the fate of his people, the people with the **eternal smile**.

She knew **Antrem was Accarus's twin brother**, separated by the disaster of **The Great Strike** years before. She knew her husband had been called for a greater purpose — something he could not refuse, even if it meant leaving his wife and only child.

But Vreina also knew this world was **cruel**.

Will of Grin — though its goals were noble — was a fugitive organization hunted by the Commonwealth and viewed with suspicion by other kingdoms.

**Antrem**, with the Lei Firage blood flowing weakly in his veins, was a **valuable target**.

So Vreina did something.

Weeks after Antrem's departure, **an old man came to Chenalia**.

His body was thin and tall, with faint scales on some parts of his skin — the mark of dragonborn blood, long diluted. His eyes were sharp, but there was **gentleness** there.

The old man was **<a href="/Kopling">Kopling</a>** — an old acquaintance of Vreina from her youth, a wanderer who had once been an adventurer.

They had met by chance years before, and Vreina had kept his name as a **backup**, in case something happened. Now, something had happened.

Inside the house, pouring tea for her guest, Vreina explained that Antrem had gone and she could not stop him — but she could not let Vya live without protection.

Kopling understood Vreina's worry that **Will of Grin** might come looking for her. But Vreina admitted she was worried about many things — especially because **Vya had inherited something from her bloodline**: **fire**.

She had seen small flashes in Vya's eyes when she was angry. Sparks when she cried.

Kopling asked if it was the fire of **Lei Firage**. Vreina confirmed — but said that was not what she feared most.

With a trembling voice, Vreina told him about **ancient records** left by her family — records about the **true Lei Firage descendants**, about fire that could not be controlled, about **children who burned from within** because of their own blood.

She did not want Vya to become one of them. She did not want to see her daughter burn alive from a legacy she never asked for.

Kopling realized this was about protecting Vya **from herself**.

Vreina did not know when the fire would fully awaken — perhaps tomorrow, perhaps next year, perhaps never. But if it did, she wanted Vya to be in a safe place with someone who could watch over her — not just from enemies, but from **within herself**.

Kopling admitted he was no wizard and could not control fire. But Vreina insisted he did not need to control it — only to **accept it**.

She chose Kopling because he was the only person she knew who would not see Vya as a **monster**. Kopling himself had dragonborn blood and knew what it was like to be different — to have something inside him that could not be explained.

After a long silence — with Vya's small laughter chasing butterflies outside — **Kopling finally promised to protect her**.

He would teach her how to survive, read tracks, use a bow. And when the fire awakened, he would be there — not to extinguish it, but to make sure Vya knew the fire was **not a curse**.

Vreina smiled bitterly, but there was relief there.

But Kopling still felt something was being hidden.

He asked why Vreina only asked him to protect Vya — not to keep her away from Will of Grin. And whether she knew something she was not saying.

Vreina sighed deeply.

She told him that before leaving, **Antrem had told her about Accarus and the grand plan of Will of Grin**. They were looking for someone with **Lei Firage blood strong enough** — not to be a weapon, but to be **hope**.

They believed that only a **true descendant of Lei Firage** could break the eternal smile curse — only **pure fire** could burn the curse away.

If that was true, then **Vya might be the answer they were looking for**.

Vreina feared they would take her and burden her with the hope of an entire people on shoulders still too young. If they came now and forced her to become something she was not ready for, **the fire would devour her from within** because she was too young to bear such a burden.

Kopling took Vreina's hand and promised to protect her — to keep her away from Will of Grin until she was old enough to choose her own path.

Vreina then whispered about her dream the night before — **fire burning the village, and Vya at its center, not as a victim but as its source**.

She did not know if it was a premonition or a nightmare. But if it truly happened, **Kopling promised he would be there** — pulling her out of the fire, telling her it was not her fault.

Vreina smiled for the last time and said Kopling was a good man.

But Kopling shook his head, admitting he was only an old man who could not bear to see a child suffer.

## Echo Sam Hurls

**~700 AV — Flash-Forward to Campaign Present**

One of the strongest **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Echoes">Echoes</a>** was **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>**.

As a bard, his life was music. His melody was everything.

When his soul was sucked into **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Simon%20Greenbottle">Simon</a>** without purification, all the songs he had never created, all the melodies interrupted mid-stream, all the grudges against the sour note of betrayal — all of it was **trapped inside him**, creating a resonance that could not rest.

Centuries after that ritual, **Echo Sam Hurls found his way out**.

He was drawn to **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>** — a place with a strong connection to **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>**, the student of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf</a>**, whose teachings he had heard in other Echoes inside Simon. Perhaps he was searching for something that reminded him of Olaf. Perhaps he was just looking for a place with enough emotional energy to make him "alive" again.

In Ghuffghuff, **Echo Sam took a horrifying form**.

He became a pale creature with many human-like heads. Each head was a fragment of someone he had met on his journeys — faces recorded in his memory, now spinning in a horrible circle atop one body.

The creature sang, and its song was a **trap**.

Anyone who heard the melody would be caught in an illusion — trapped in an unreal version of Ghuffghuff, slowly drained of energy until they became a dried corpse like **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Yahanda">Yahanda</a>** and **<a href="/Faska">Faska</a>**.

The creature was not evil in the ordinary sense. It was only **hungry** — hungry for life, for energy, for something to fill the emptiness left by a fractured soul.

And it sang. It kept singing — the same song it never finished at the altar — hoping that one day, someone would come and **play the final note for it**.

When **<a href="/oversee/pcs">The Five Sticks</a>** finally faced the creature at the **teater of Ghuffghuff**, they realized ordinary violence could not defeat it. Every head that was cut off grew back. Because it was not a flesh-and-blood creature — it was an **Echo**, and Echoes cannot be killed with a sword.

**<a href="/oversee/pcs/Shin%20Van%20Hel">HVS</a>** — with the soul-thirsting sword **<a href="/oversee/items/Swaith%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Soul-Thirsting%20Blade">Swaith</a>** — drove the blade into the creature's heart.

Swaith — which could absorb and release souls — did something unexpected: it **released the souls trapped inside the creature's body**, freeing them.

And for a moment, the creature **changed**.

For the first time in centuries, **Sam Hurls appeared in his true form** — a slightly chubby man with wavy black hair, purple and red clothing that had once been his signature, and a lute in his hand. His eyes glowed faintly grey — a sign that he was no longer a living human — but for a moment, he could **speak**.

He did not have time to say much.

From a distance, an **arrow** flew fast, and Sam Hurls returned to death — or perhaps returned to Simon, or perhaps to somewhere else unknown.

But in that instant, the truth was revealed: **one of The Initial Six had not truly left**.

He was only waiting — echoing between worlds — waiting for someone brave enough to listen to his unfinished song.

Other Echoes might also be waiting in other places.

- **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atreus%20Grin">Atreus Grin</a>** might echo in the forests he once protected, or in the ruins of the fallen Grin Kingdom.
- **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gzirmo%20Kopling">Gzirmo Kopling</a>** might echo in the mountains of **Elis**, near the clan he left behind.
- **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Atlan%20Nouveau">Atlan Nouveau</a>** might echo in the **<a href="/The%20Observatorium">Observatorium</a>** of **<a href="/Kota%20Juto">Kota Juto</a>**, or on the shore where he first found **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Reins">Reins</a>**.
- **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf Guirela</a>** might echo in the villages he once protected, or within **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>** and later **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Husgus">Husgus</a>**, who inherited his teachings without knowing where they came from.

They wait, these Echoes. Waiting for the moment to speak again — to finish what was interrupted at that altar.

And **<a href="/oversee/factions/Keeper%20of%20Secret">Keeper of Secret</a>** — the organization founded by **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Mira%20Nouveau">Mira Nouveau</a>** — continues to guard this secret. Not only the betrayal of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Alejandro">Lei Alejandro</a>**, but also the **hope** that one day, in ways they do not yet understand, the Echoes might find **peace**.

## Gordon Meets Husgus

**~690 AV**

Forty years after the war ended, **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gordon">Gordon</a>** — now ancient, his age approaching five centuries — still lived in his hut in **<a href="/Hutan%20Domido">Hutan Domido</a>**.

His body could barely move. But his eyes remained sharp. His days were spent sitting in front of the hut, watching the forest, occasionally singing the old melody **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>** had taught him.

One morning, when fog still covered the trees, Gordon saw something strange.

Beneath the old tree where he usually sat, **a small body lay**.

With difficulty he approached, leaning on his wooden staff.

The figure was **a small child without clothes**. A tiny body, perhaps eight or nine years old, with pale skin and thin hair. No wounds. No signs of violence.

The child simply lay there — as if **just fallen from the sky**.

Slowly, inch by inch, Gordon dragged the child into his hut. He was too old and weak to carry him.

For days he cared for him — changing compresses, giving him water and finely mashed food. The child had no fever, no illness. He only slept deeply, like a statue.

When the child finally woke, **his eyes stared at Gordon emptily**.

Without curiosity. Without fear. Without anything. Like uncarved glass.

With a hoarse voice — like someone speaking for the first time — he muttered about who he was. He shook his head when asked. He only remembered **darkness, fire, and someone screaming**.

He did not know his name. Did not know where he came from. Did not know anything about this world.

The only word he could say, over and over in his sleep, was **"Husgus."**

Gordon looked at the child for a long time.

In those empty eyes, he saw something familiar. Not as a human — but as a **mirror**. Like looking into himself as a child — orphaned, alone in this forest.

Slowly, he said that the child might not know who he was, but he knew his name — and that was enough to start.

With worn cloth he wrapped the small body, gave him the name **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Husgus">Husgus</a>**, and said the child would stay here with him until he found his answer.

For the first time, there was a little light in Husgus's eyes — perhaps a question, perhaps hope.

Gordon knew one thing: this world was cruel, and this child needed someone to protect him — just as **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Olaf%20Guirela">Olaf</a>** had once protected him.

For the next **five years**, **Gordon trained Husgus**.

His ancient body moved slowly, but his mind was still sharp. He taught many things: how to **hunt**, how to **read animal tracks**, how to **set traps**, how to **use a bow**. And the same philosophy Olaf had once taught him — about **protecting the weak**, about **standing tall even when the whole world thinks you are mad**.

**Husgus learned with astonishing speed.**

Within months, he could read tracks better than Gordon. Within a year, he could shoot a flying bird with terrifying accuracy.

But the strangest thing: **he never got sick**.

And at night, when he slept, he often muttered in a language Gordon did not recognize — a language that sounded ancient, with a strange rhythm, like a mantra.

Gordon knew something was wrong. This child was **not an ordinary human**.

But he did not care. What mattered was that **Husgus had a good heart** — and that was worth more than any bloodline.

One night by the campfire, Gordon asked if Husgus remembered anything about his past — about before he came here.

Husgus shook his head. He only remembered **darkness, someone screaming, and a blazing fire**.

Gordon sighed and said the child might not be from this place — might be from somewhere far away. But that did not matter. What mattered was what he did **now**.

Husgus asked what he should do.

Gordon answered with a bitter smile: **he should live, learn, become strong, and when the time came, choose for himself who he was**. He was old and would not last much longer — but Husgus had time, and he should use it well.

## The Death of Gordon

**~695 AV**

**<a href="/oversee/npcs/Malakor">Malakor</a>** — one of the few people who still remembered Gordon — was a wizard who had sided with **Gudameng** during the war. A fanatic who believed **fried noodles were sacred**.

But what few knew: **Malakor was an agent of the Commonwealth**.

He had been placed in Gudameng to ensure the fires of war kept burning, to report every development, and to **silence anyone who tried to reveal the truth** — including the truth about the project at **Gunung Rayu**.

During the war, Malakor had watched how Gordon nearly exposed all their plans. He had watched the **Noisy Rhino** gather evidence, speak with witnesses, and nearly tear open the veil that had been kept tight for centuries.

Though the evidence had been destroyed and the witnesses silenced, Malakor knew: **Gordon was still alive, still knew, and as long as Gordon lived, the truth could never truly die**.

After the war ended, Malakor secluded himself in an old tower at the edge of **Hutan Domido**, near the ruins of the old fortress. He gathered his followers — former warriors from both sides who could not return to normal life, who still held grudges, who could be bought with promises of power or revenge.

And he waited for the right moment.

One night in **695 AV**, while **Gordon** sat in front of his hut singing the old melody, **Malakor and his men attacked**.

They came like shadows. Dozens of armed men.

**<a href="/oversee/pcs/Husgus">Husgus</a>** — who was hunting deer in another part of the forest — heard strange sounds. He ran home. But he was **too late**.

**Gordon fought bravely.**

The **<a href="/oversee/items/Gustaero%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Swan%20Bow">Gustaero bow</a>** in his hands loosed arrow after arrow, and several of Malakor's men fell.

But there were too many.

And when **Gordon saw the faces of the attackers**, his heart broke.

They were people from both villages — people he had once saved, once spoken to, once looked in the eyes, once hoped could live in peace.

Now they were **executioners**.

With a laugh, **Malakor stepped forward**. He said peace could not last forever. He said the truth Gordon sought was more dangerous than war. He said silence was not enough — **Gordon had to die**.

**Malakor's dagger pierced Gordon's chest.**

In his final moments, **Gordon saw Husgus's silhouette appear from behind the trees**.

He wanted to scream at the child to run. But his voice was gone.

**Husgus screamed** — not a scream of fear, but of **pure rage**.

He grabbed the bow Gordon had given him and loosed an arrow at Malakor. The arrow struck the wizard's shoulder, making him stagger.

Malakor turned furiously, asked who the child was, and raised his hand with magic gathering at his fingertips.

**Husgus drew another arrow** — but Malakor was faster.

The spell flew, striking **Husgus's small body**.

And then **something happened**.

**Husgus's body shone**.

**Golden light radiated from within him**. For a moment, **Malakor saw something that made him stagger back in terror** — not a small child, but **something else**. Something that should not exist.

Husgus fell to his knees, body trembling. He felt something **changing** inside him — not pain, but **transformation**.

His bones cracked. His muscles contracted. His body **shrank** — smaller, lighter.

When the light faded, he was still a child — but **smaller than before**.

In the chaos, **Husgus grabbed Gordon's body** and fled into the forest.

Malakor and his men chased. But **Hutan Domido was Husgus's home**. He knew every hollow, every hiding place, every secret path.

He vanished like a ghost.

Beneath the old tree where he had first been found, **Husgus buried Gordon**.

His teacher's body felt heavy, though he was only a small child. With his own hands he dug the grave, tears falling.

When the grave was finished, he sat beside it all night.

In his hands, the **Gustaero bow** felt warm — as if the bow knew its owner had left, and was now **waiting for a new owner**.

The next day, he began **tracking Malakor**.

His small body turned out to be an advantage — he could move faster, more agilely, harder to see. Malakor's tracks were not difficult to follow; the old wizard was too confident, too sure that no one would chase him.

**Malakor's tower** stood at the edge of the forest, near the ruins of the old fortress.

Around it, **corpses hung** — Malakor's followers who had failed in their tasks, or perhaps victims of the wizard's mad rituals. The stench stung Husgus's nose, but he did not flinch.

He slipped in through a crack in the stone wall.

At the top of the tower, **Malakor was performing a ritual**.

Black candles burned around him, and in the center of the circle lay a large book with a **human skin cover**.

Husgus did not understand what he was seeing. But he knew one thing: **this was the moment**.

He drew Gordon's bow — the bow too large for his small body — and aimed straight at Malakor's heart.

But before he could release the arrow, **the wizard turned**.

His eyes widened at the sight of Husgus. Then he laughed — saying that a small child with a tiny body could not possibly defeat him.

He raised his hand, magic gathering.

**Husgus loosed the arrow.**

At the same moment, **Malakor cast his spell**.

**Husgus's arrow flew straight into Malakor's chest, piercing his heart.**

But **the wizard's spell also reached Husgus** — not as a direct attack, but as a **wave of energy** that swept the room.

And **Husgus's body shone again** — **brighter than before**.

**Golden light radiated from within him**, illuminating the entire room.

**Something released** — like chains that had bound him, now broken.

His body trembled, changed, **shrank even smaller**.

When the light faded, he was **a halfling** — a small body, short, with slightly pointed ears and thick hair.

**Malakor lay dead on the floor**, Husgus's arrow still in his chest.

Husgus stood, swaying, and picked up **Gordon's bow**.

He looked at his own small body, then laughed bitterly, whispering that **this was him** — this was his true form.

He did not know what it meant. But he knew one thing: **Gordon was gone, and he had to continue**.

**He burned the tower** — along with all the evil books inside.

The fire rose high, lighting the night sky.

**Husgus walked away**, leaving the ruins behind, heading toward a wider world.

On the grip of Gordon's bow, he carved simple words:

> *"Here lies Gordon, the Noisy Rhino. He saw the truth, and the world thought him mad. But he never stopped shouting. I will not forget you."*

After leaving **Hutan Domido**, **Husgus wandered without purpose**, searching for answers about who he was. His small body was both a burden and an advantage — he could slip into places ordinary people could not enter, but he also had to face a world that looked down on halflings.

## Aftermath

Years later, in **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>** and **<a href="/oversee/locations/Gudameng%20Town">Gudameng</a>**, the name **Gordon** was almost forgotten.

But the **melody** that **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Sam%20Hurls">Sam Hurls</a>** had taught Gordon — and Gordon had taught to the villagers — **lived on**.

Children sang it without knowing what it meant. Mothers hummed it while putting babies to sleep. Farmers whistled it while working in the fields.

The melody seeped into the soil, into the trees, into the air.

**Centuries later**, when **<a href="/oversee/pcs">The Five Sticks</a>** came to **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>**, the melody would **call back Echo Sam Hurls** — Sam would appear in horrifying form, waiting for someone brave enough to listen to his unfinished song.

At **<a href="/Gunung%20Rayu">Gunung Rayu</a>**, the **secret project of the Commonwealth continued for centuries**.

What they dug up in the ancient underground city, what artifacts they found — **no one knew**. Because the war they had created had successfully distracted everyone.

In **Hutan Domido**, beneath the old tree, **a bow stood planted in the ground** — a grave marker for a hero the world never acknowledged.

But the fire of his struggle **continued to burn** — in **<a href="/oversee/pcs/Husgus">Husgus</a>**, in the melody that never died, and in the hearts of those who believed that the truth, though buried, would never truly be lost.

Because **Gordon had taught**: sometimes, all that is needed is **one voice loud enough** to disturb the sleep of the powerful.

## The Truth Beyond the Player-Facing Text

The player-facing text above is the **Keeper of Secret's archive entry** for the Power Surge era, supplemented by **Grin oral history** and **Gordon's testimony**. It is accurate in its broad strokes — but it omits several truths.

### What the Party Does Not Know Yet

**The Gunung Rayu project is not just an excavation.**

It is a **Cycle Clan seed vault** — a smaller version of the **<a href="/oversee/locations/The%20Underground%20Ark">Bahtera Bawah Tanah</a>**. Inside: a fragment of the **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Edisi%20Law">Edisi Law</a>**, a **Topaju fragment**, and a **cryo-pod with a preserved Cycle Clan member**. This is how **<a href="/Valerius%20Thorne">Valerius Thorne</a>** learned about the Edisi Law and the override clause. He found it here.

**The Gruffgud War was engineered by the Commonwealth.**

Not just "encouraged" — **engineered**. The Commonwealth agents who started the war were following orders from the top of the Triase. The war was designed to last centuries, to keep both villages weak and distracted, while the excavation at Gunung Rayu proceeded.

**Gordon was right about everything.**

He was not mad. He was not demented. He was **the only person in the region who saw the truth** — and he was silenced, exiled, and mocked for it. His grave is still in Hutan Domido. His bow still stands as a marker. And his voice — the voice of the **Noisy Rhino** — still echoes in the memory of anyone who ever heard him.

**Malakor was not acting alone.**

He was an agent of the **Commonwealth** — specifically, of **<a href="/Valerius%20Thorne">Valerius Thorne</a>**. Valerius personally ordered Gordon's death. He knew Gordon had been investigating Gunung Rayu. He knew Gordon was the last living witness to the conspiracy. And he knew that as long as Gordon lived, the truth could resurface.

**Valerius's order:** *"Kill the old dwarf. Burn the hut. Destroy every trace of his investigation. And if the child survives... bring him to me."*

Malakor failed the last part. He did not bring Husgus to Valerius. He tried to kill him instead.

**Husgus's transformation was not caused by Malakor's spell.**

Malakor's spell triggered it — but the transformation was always going to happen. Husgus is **HVS-01** — the **Human Vessel Shell** designed to hold **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Overseer%20%26%20The%20Architect">the Architect</a>**. The shell was never filled. In the emptiness, a new soul grew — **Husgus's soul**.

The golden light was not magic. It was **the Architect's old structure showing through** — the Inbu-inspired biology that Wiseman had built into the shell, now visible beneath the surface of a mortal life.

**When Malakor's spell hit Husgus, it triggered the shell's emergency protocol:** *"If the shell is ever damaged and the vessel is still empty, collapse to the smallest possible form and seal the golden cracks."*

Husgus became a halfling because the shell needed a smaller body to contain the damage.

**Husgus has no idea what he is.**

He thinks his transformation was a curse — some random magical backlash from Malakor's spell. He does not know he is a **vessel**. He does not know his body was designed to hold a god.

**The melody Sam taught Gordon is a key.**

It is not just a song. It is a **resonance** — a specific frequency that can attract **Echoes**. When it is sung in **<a href="/Ghuffghuff">Ghuffghuff</a>**, it draws **Echo Sam** to the village. When it is sung in other places, it may draw other Echoes.

The children of Ghuffghuff have been singing it for centuries without knowing what it does. They have been **calling Echo Sam** without realizing it.

**Vreina is still alive.**

She was not killed by Will of Grin. She was not killed by the Commonwealth. She was **taken** — by agents of **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Lei%20Firsa">Lei Firsa</a>**, who wanted to know what Antrem had told her about the Gemhearts. She is being held in a **secret Commonwealth facility in Elis**.

The facility is the real reason **<a href="/Valerius%20Thorne">Valerius</a>** is "reconstructing" **<a href="/Chenalia">Chenalia</a>**. The village is a cover for an excavation.

**Kopling is not just an old wanderer.**

He is **<a href="/oversee/npcs/Gzirmo%20Kopling">Gzirmo Kopling</a>**'s distant relative — from the same dragonborn bloodline that produced the tinker of **<a href="/oversee/lore/The%20Initial%20Six">The Initial Six</a>**. He carries the same diluted blood, the same faint connection to the ancient dragons. He knows more about the Kopling lineage than he lets on.

He also knows **the secret of the Vessel Shells** — though he does not know that Husgus is one. He suspects. He has been watching. And he has been waiting.

**Accarus's true mission for Antrem.**

When **Accarus** came to **Chenalia** to take **Antrem** away, he told Vreina it was for a "greater purpose." The truth is darker: **Accarus needed Antrem's blood** — the weak Lei Firage blood — to perform a ritual. He believed that mixing his own blood with Antrem's could create a **new spark** — a fire strong enough to one day challenge the true heirs of Lei Firage.

Antrem did not know this. He went willingly. He believed he was helping his brother.

He has been in **Maw Desert** ever since — not as a prisoner, but as a **guest who cannot leave**. He is not allowed to see Vya. He is not allowed to write to her. He is not allowed to know what his blood is being used for.

He is a **resource**, not a brother.

**The truth about Antrem's "simple" nature.**

Antrem is not actually simple-minded. He is **genuinely simple**. His simplicity is the result of Hildag's experiments, The Great Strike, and the separation from his twin. He is not drugged. He is not pretending. He is a kind, quiet man who has been used his whole life.

**The truth about Darvin Thorne.**

**Darvin is the True Heir of Lei Roiga — by blood, not by stone.**

He carries the **claim** to **Topaju**. He does not carry the stone itself.

**Lei Alejandro holds Topaju.** He found it centuries ago in the ruins of a Lei Roiga temple. He has kept it hidden ever since. He will give it to the party if they prove they are worthy.

**Valerius is hunting Darvin for a lie.** He believes Darvin carries Topaju. He is wrong. He has been hunting his brother for years, trying to kill him and claim the Gemheart. He will not find it. He will only find his brother's corpse — and the knowledge that he killed him for nothing.

**If Valerius kills Darvin, nothing will happen.** Topaju will still be with Lei Alejandro. The earth will not destabilize. Valerius will have killed his brother for nothing.

**Darvin can sense Topaju.** He can feel it. He can hear it. He has been hearing it his whole life. He does not know what it is. He only knows he can feel the earth — he can sense the stone that is not his.

**Darvin does not know he is the True Heir.** He does not know Topaju exists. He only knows he can feel the earth — and that it has been calling to him his whole life.

**If Darvin survives and meets Lei Alejandro**, he will feel the Topaju. He will know it is real. He will know he was not crazy. He will weep. He will say: *"I thought I was broken. I was just listening."*

### The Major Contradictions to Resolve

**Contradiction 1: Gordon's Age at Death (Resolved)**

- This document says **Gordon was over 400 years old in 650 AV** and **died in 695 AV** at around **470-500 years old**.
- **`Gordon.md`** (character file) says he was **over 500 years old when he died** in 695 AV.
- **`Timeline of Oversee.md`** says he was born **~180 AV** and was 515+ when he died.

**Resolution: Gordon was born ~180 AV.** He was **over 500 years old** when he died in **695 AV**. The Gustaero bow **slowed his aging dramatically**. The "grew into a young man" section in 240 AV is a **flashback** to his youth — not what was happening in 240 AV. By 240 AV, he was 60 — but the Gustaero bow had slowed his aging. He looked young. He was not young.

**Contradiction 2: The Olaf/Sam Meeting Date (Resolved)**

- I placed the Olaf/Sam meeting at **~190 AV** in `Initial Six Era.md`.
- This document says **Gordon grew into a strong young man in 240 AV** — 50 years later.

**Resolution: Gordon was born ~180 AV.** The Olaf/Sam meeting happened when he was ~10 years old — around **190 AV**. By 240 AV, he was 60 — old for a human, but not old for someone touched by Gustaero. The Gustaero bow **slowed his aging dramatically**.
