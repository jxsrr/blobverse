> [!info] The Age of the First God
> When the calendar was rewritten. When the bloodline was founded. When the truth became a lie.

## The New Calendar

The year **1 AV** — *After Valla* — began in silence.

**[[Lei Firsa]]** stood at the altar of **[[Universitas Tarakita|Danau Kuno]]**. **[[Simon Greenbottle]]** sat before her — not moving, not breathing, but not dead. His body was still warm. His eyes were still open. But he was no longer there.

He was the **[[The Valla|Valla]]** now. His soul stretched across the world, holding back the hunger of the **[[The Overseer & The Architect|Overseer]]**. He would hold that burden for as long as he could. And when he finally broke, someone else would take his place.

Lei Firsa looked at him for a long time. Then she turned away.

She had what she wanted. She had **immortality** through Simon. She had **power** through the **[[The Gemhearts|Gemhearts]]**. She had **control** over the cycle that kept the world alive.

But there was one thing that still bothered her.

The **legendary weapons** — the blades and relics Simon had collected over his lifetime — were proof that Simon had existed. Each weapon had a story. Each story connected to a name — the name of a Halfling shepherd who had been too kind for the world.

**[[Zenahhi — The Loyal Blade|Zenahhi]]**. **[[Rayio — The Wind Blade|Rayio]]**. **[[Kkana — The Stone Greatsword|Kkana]]**. **[[Frostfang — The Ice Fang|Frostfang]]**. **[[Terrde — The Patient Blade|Terrde]]**. **[[Swaith — The Soul-Thirsting Blade|Swaith]]**. And **[[Gustaero — The Swan Bow|Gustaero]]** — the bow that was not a blade, but a relic.

She could not destroy them. They were ancient — older than the Klan, older than the Cycle Clan. Too strong to destroy. Too dangerous to leave in the wrong hands.

But she could **lose** them.

She could scatter them across the world — to places no one would look, in pieces no one could reassemble — and let the world forget.

She began that night.

## The Scattering of the Weapons

### Gustaero — The South Sea, Harka

The first weapon she took from the altar was **[[Gustaero — The Swan Bow|Gustaero]]**.

The longbow was pale, almost transparent — its invisible string hummed softly as she held it, as if in protest.

She remembered the story Simon had told her. He had received it from an old woman in **[[Wind's Rest]]** — **Edna Gustaero** — who had trusted him because he was "a good person." Too many people knew about that meeting. Too many witnesses were still alive.

She traveled alone to the southern coast of **Harka** — to the highest cliff above Wind's Rest. She stood at the edge of the chasm, the sea wind whipping her face, the waves breaking on the rocks a hundred meters below.

> *"In the place where Simon received this trust,"* she said, *"the trust will end."*

She threw Gustaero into the sea.

The bow fell, spun, and disappeared into white foam. Lei Firsa smiled.

> *"Let the sea forget it. As the world will forget Simon."*

### Rayio — The Garberro Forest, Pirizia

**[[Rayio — The Wind Blade|Rayio]]** was a light sword with a blue-silver blade that made almost no sound when swung. Lei Firsa carried it across the sea to **Pirizia**, to the **[[Hutan Garberro|Garberro Forest]]** — a forbidden forest said to be haunted by restless spirits and creatures from the Gap that slipped through occasionally.

The trees here grew crooked. Their roots reached like wrinkled hands grasping at the soil. Thick fog covered every corner. Strange sounds echoed in the distance.

Lei Firsa walked to the center of the forest, where sunlight almost never broke through the canopy. She threw Rayio into the dark undergrowth, among the roots that seemed to move.

> *"Dance with the wind there,"* she said. *"Or be eaten by demons."*

Rayio fell with a rustle and was silent. Lei Firsa left without looking back.

### Kkana — The Misty Mountains, Elis

**[[Kkana — The Stone Greatsword|Kkana]]** was a heavy, sturdy greatsword made of black iron patterned like stone layers. It felt impossibly heavy in Lei Firsa's hands — as if reluctant to be moved. She had to hire porters to carry it to the ship, then to the **Misty Mountains** in **Elis** — the highest range on the continent, their peaks always shrouded in cloud.

The climb took days. Lei Firsa nearly gave up several times, but her ambition was greater than her exhaustion. At last, she reached the highest peak, among giant stones standing like natural monuments. Cold wind bit at her skin. Thin snow began to fall.

She planted Kkana in a crack between the stones and stepped back.

> *"You like stone? Then stay here."*

Kkana stood upright, as if in agreement. Lei Firsa descended without looking back.

### Frostfang — Frosthaven Peak, Northern Pirizia

**[[Frostfang — The Ice Fang|Frostfang]]** was the most dangerous of the six. Its blade — carved from the tooth of a giant ice dragon — radiated a cold that froze the air around it. When Lei Firsa touched it, frost immediately coated her hands. She barely managed to carry it; the cold pierced her skin and made her bones ache.

She had to move quickly. She carried Frostfang to northern **Pirizia**, to **[[Frosthaven Peak]]** — the highest mountain on the continent, where eternal snow and ice storms never stopped. Here, the temperature was far below freezing, and the wind blew so hard it could tear flesh.

Lei Firsa stood at the cliff edge, looking down at the white chasm below. With all her strength, she threw Frostfang into the storm. The great axe fell, spun, and was buried in thick snow within seconds.

> *"You will be comfortable here,"* she said, her breath freezing in the air. *"Freeze forever."*

### Zenahhi — The Canyon of Lament, Eastern Rioquan

**[[Zenahhi — The Loyal Blade|Zenahhi]]** was the simplest of the six — a classic longsword with a shining blade, a dark blue hilt, and perfect balance. But its simplicity was what made it dangerous. The sword was too **good**. Too **noble**. Lei Firsa hated it.

She carried Zenahhi to **Rioquan**, to the **[[Ngarai Ratapan|Canyon of Lament]]** — the deepest canyon on the continent, its bottom said to have never been touched by sunlight. Legend said that thousands of years ago, the gods wept here, and their tears carved the canyon. No one had ever descended to the bottom and returned alive.

Lei Firsa stood at the canyon's edge, the wind howling from the depths. She threw Zenahhi into the darkness.

> *"Your loyalty will die here,"* she whispered.

Zenahhi fell, bounced off the stone walls, and vanished into the dark. Lei Firsa was satisfied.

### Terrde — The Ruins of the Ancient City, Harka

**[[Terrde — The Patient Blade|Terrde]]** was different. Its blade was darker, thicker, designed to wait and strike with efficiency. Lei Firsa felt something **patient** in this sword — as if it did not mind waiting for as long as it took.

She carried Terrde to **Harka**, to the ruins of an ancient city destroyed in **[[The Rapture]]** centuries before. The city had once been the greatest center of magical civilization in its era, before the light-eating comet came and devoured all its wizards. Now only rubble remained, and bones, and a suffocating silence.

Lei Firsa entered the deepest underground chamber, among shelves of moldy books and useless magical tools. In the corner, she saw a pile of bones — perhaps the last wizard to die here. She placed Terrde in the lap of the skeleton, as if handing the sword to a new guardian.

> *"You like waiting?"* she said. *"Then wait here forever."*

## The Rewritten History

With the six blades and the Swan Bow scattered, Lei Firsa returned to **[[Universitas Tarakita|Danau Kuno]]**.

Simon's body still sat on the altar — not moving, not breathing. Lei Firsa looked at him one last time. Not with love. With satisfaction.

> *"You will be remembered as a god,"* she whispered. *"Not as Simon Greenbottle, the Halfling shepherd who was too kind. You will be the Valla, the Guardian of the World. And I… I will continue."*

She declared the year **1 After Valla**. The new calendar began. The old history was erased.

According to the version of history written by scribes loyal to her, **Simon Greenbottle never existed**. Instead, the world knew of **"Lei the Great"** — a hero who ascended to godhood after his great mission. And **Lei Firsa** herself, through the distortion of history over time, was remembered as a **wise and powerful male wizard** — the founder of **[[Klan Lei]]**.

The weapons waited in their hiding places — in the sea, in the forbidden forest, on the mountain peak, at the bottom of the canyon, in the ruins, in the underground temple. They waited. They called. They would keep waiting until someone worthy found them.

## The Bloodline of the First God

**~01–20 AV**

Years passed. The world turned in its silent orbit. **[[Lei Firsa]]** — now in full control of the **[[The Cycle Clan|Cycle Clan]]** and the **[[The Gemhearts|Gemhearts]]** — watched from a distance as her power grew.

**[[Simon Greenbottle|Simon]]** still sat on the altar. His physical body had not changed. His consciousness stretched between the mortal world and the presence of the Overseer. Every five hundred years, the cycle would be prepared again — the maintenance ritual that had to be performed so the world remained safe from cosmic hunger. But Lei Firsa had made sure that the cycle would never move away from Simon as long as she lived.

From Lei Firsa's womb came the descendants who would become known as **[[Klan Lei]]**.

She gave birth to **four children**. Each grew up in an environment filled with knowledge of the Gemhearts, of the ritual, and of their father who sat eternal on the altar. Each inherited something no one else in Oversee possessed: **pure elemental power**, flowing in their blood like a second pulse.

- **[[Lei Wendigo]]** — the firstborn. Wind. Received **[[Emelanica]]**.
- **[[Lei Firage]]** — the second. Fire. Received **[[Rubion]]**.
- **[[Lei Watigi]]** — the third. Water. Received **[[Safayer]]**.
- **[[Lei Roiga]]** — the fourth. Earth. Received **[[Topaju]]**.

But no one knew the truth behind this miracle.

The power inherited by Lei Firsa's four children was the fruit of **Simon's final love and sacrifice** — the Halfling who was now trapped as the Valla. From a distance, from behind the veil of reality that separated the mortal world from cosmic darkness, Simon could still feel the pulse of his children's lives. He could not move. He could not speak. He could not touch them. But he could **channel** something — pure Ambitio-Ether that he had absorbed as the Valla, flowing through the blood bond that was never severed.

A father's helpless love — a father who wanted to protect his children from a distance — became the conduit connecting the altar to the wombs that bore Lei's descendants. Every time a child was born from his bloodline, Simon shed a drop of his essence, giving them a spark of pure elemental power that no one else could ever possess.

## The Rule of Succession

After making Simon the Valla and ensuring that his descendants were granted pure magical power through their blood bond with their father, **[[Lei Firsa]]** took the next step.

With the Gemhearts now in her possession — the legacy of the **Cycle Clan** she had fully controlled — she divided the four primordial stones among her four children.

Then she established a rule that could not be broken: **the four Gemheart fragments could only be passed down to the pure-blooded firstborn of each elemental line**. Only the firstborn of Lei Wendigo, Lei Firage, Lei Watigi, and Lei Roiga could inherit their ancestral Gemheart.

The reason was simple, and cruel: **the blood of the firstborn was closest to Simon's own blood** — Simon, who still sat eternal on the altar as the Valla. The purer Simon's blood flowed in a descendant, the stronger their bond with the Gemheart, and the greater the elemental power they could channel.

This rule became the foundation of Klan Lei's magical aristocracy — and the source of countless intrigues and conflicts that would color their history for generations.

## The Silent Guardians

Generations passed. **Klan Lei** grew into the most feared and respected magical aristocracy in all Oversee. They did not need to learn magic — they were magic itself. Grand palaces were built on the land that had once been the **Observatory of the Ancient Lake**. Nobles from other kingdoms came to bow, begging for protection or alliance. Klan Lei stood at the top of the world — rulers who did not need to raise a weapon to show their power.

But behind the splendor, Klan Lei held the darkest secret.

They knew that the source of their power — the pure elemental force flowing in every Lei descendant — was tied to **Simon's existence as the Valla**. As long as Simon sat on the altar, holding back the Overseer's hunger, his essence continued to flow to them. If Simon were ever replaced by a new Valla — if the maintenance ritual were performed and Simon's soul finally released — that source of power would vanish forever. Klan Lei would fall from their throne of magical aristocracy, becoming ordinary people no more special than a farmer in a field.

So Klan Lei guarded the cycle with extreme care. Every five hundred years, when the time of maintenance approached, they made sure no one disturbed Simon. No new volunteers were allowed near the altar. Loyal guards stood day and night, protecting the sacred chamber where Simon sat eternal. They did not perform the ritual of replacement — they only **waited**, hoping Simon could endure forever.

But nature has its own laws. A soul, even one stretched into a cosmic seal, eventually cracks. **Simon began to show signs of exhaustion.** His divided consciousness sometimes shifted too far to the cosmic side, leaving him unaware for months at a time. The Ambitio-Ether he channeled to his descendants began to fluctuate, unstable.

Klan Lei realized they had to do something to preserve their bloodline.

## The Darkest Secret

Here the darkest secret was revealed.

When a Lei descendant gave birth to **twins**, a strange phenomenon occurred: the destiny-soul that should have been whole for one generation was split in two. The pure elemental power that should have been inherited fully by one heir was divided, so each child received only half — or less — of the full potential. Some twin children were even born with no magic at all, like ordinary people.

For Klan Lei, this was a disaster. A magical aristocracy could not have weak heirs.

So a dark tradition emerged, kept tightly from the outside world. When a mother gave birth to twins, one baby would be taken immediately after birth. In the silence of the night, without a cry, without resistance, that baby would be killed. Its blood would be dripped onto a small altar dedicated to Simon — not as a vile offering, but as a way of returning the split soul to its twin.

The elders of Klan Lei believed that by killing one twin, the remaining soul of the dead baby would naturally return to the living one. This process, though horrifying, was believed to reunite the divided destiny, creating a single true heir with full elemental power. The surviving child grew with maximum potential, never knowing they stood on the grave of their own twin.

This tradition lasted for generations, kept in whispers in the corridors of Klan Lei palaces. Mothers wept in silence. Fathers turned away when the baby was taken. The elders performed their duty with expressionless faces.

They called it *"purification of the bloodline"* or *"unification of destiny."*

But no euphemism could cover the truth: **Klan Lei killed its own children to maintain power.**

## The Commonwealth of United Voices

The **[[One Voice Commonwealth|Commonwealth of United Voices]]** was built on the ruins of chaos that followed decades after the disappearance of the greatest hero the world had ever known.

Simon's departure — or more precisely, his transformation into the Valla who sat eternal on the altar — created a power vacuum no one could fill. Adventurers, kingdoms, and small factions competed in uncertainty, while external threats began to resurface after being muted for so long by Simon's presence.

In the midst of this vacuum, **[[Lei Firsa]]** and her descendants took the initiative.

With Gemhearts in hand, with Simon still sitting as the source of power on the altar, and with ambition that never dimmed despite her advancing age, Lei Firsa began designing a new order. She was no longer merely the leader of the hidden Cycle Clan. She wanted to build something greater — something that would outlast her own life, something that would be a monument to her ambition and a shield for her bloodline.

The Commonwealth was formed with a structure known as the **[[The Triase|Triase]]** — a triumvirate in which each officeholder represented an essential pillar of civilization.

At the top stood the **Kanselir Agung** — the **Grand Chancellor** — the supreme leader who served as guardian of wisdom and long-term vision. The Chancellor acted as unifier, mediator, and final decision-maker after hearing the views of the other two, making the position a symbol of reason, stability, and political and spiritual trust.

Beside the Chancellor stood the **Wakil Keadilan** — the **Justice Deputy** — who led the **[[The Rose Guard|Garda Mawar]]** (Rose Guard), the institution responsible for law, courts, and internal affairs. The Justice Deputy oversaw the court system, inter-regional contracts, dispute resolution, and maintenance of the Commonwealth's legal doctrine — requiring sharp analytical thinking, high integrity, and unusual diplomatic skill.

Meanwhile, the **Wakil Pertahanan** — the **Defense Deputy** — held control of the **[[The Iron Guard|Garda Besi]]** (Iron Guard): the military, territorial defense, public security, and intelligence. The Defense Deputy was the face of the Commonwealth's power, ensuring every threat — from within or without — could be met with toughness and speed without waiting for bureaucratic decisions.

Inevitably, because of the history begun by **Lei Firsa**, the majority of high offices in the Commonwealth were held by Klan Lei descendants. What began as the personal ambition of a cast-out woman who wanted recognition had grown into a network of power that spread to every corner of government. By occupying key positions, Klan Lei could ensure their bloodline was preserved and expanded — creating a system resembling a magical aristocracy in which political power and magic reinforced each other.

Those with Simon's blood flowing in their veins, who inherited the pure elemental power of their ancestors, were naturally placed in positions that allowed them to protect family interests while still running the government. A perfect vicious circle: magical power gave them legitimacy to rule, and political power gave them protection to preserve the purity of their bloodline and the secret behind the source of their power.

A clear example of this system could be seen in the Governor of West Harka — a leader named **[[Hanzel Lei Krow]]**, a direct descendant of the **Lei Wendigo** line, heir to the pure wind magic and guardian of **[[Emelanica]]**. His appointment as governor was not merely an ordinary political decision; it was a symbolic statement that the Commonwealth recognized Klan Lei's traditional authority while binding it within a modern government structure.

## The Doctrine of Simon's Ascension

The spiritual doctrine that became the foundation of Klan Lei's power in the Commonwealth taught that the greatest hero — **Simon Greenbottle** — did not merely disappear. He **ascended to godhood**.

It was a narrative designed by **Lei Firsa** to transform the Halfling shepherd into a transcendent figure who legitimized Klan Lei's power. Simon was depicted as achieving divinity after his self-sacrifice, and to strengthen this claim, Klan Lei established the **Calendar of 1 After Valla (AV)**, erasing the old dating system and claiming that the elemental powers (wind, fire, water, earth) demonstrated by members of their clan were proof that **the blood of a god flowed in their veins** — making them a sacred family and the rightful heirs of the new guardian deity.

## The Silent Legacy of the Wild Continent

Amid the bustle of the newly born Commonwealth — where the Doctrine of Simon's Ascension was being proclaimed and Klan Lei was consolidating its power — there was another world beating to a completely different rhythm.

Across the sea, in the wild and untouched continent of **[[Elis]]**, time flowed as it had since the beginning of creation. There, among the misty peaks that pierced the sky and the valleys never trodden by mortal feet, the giant creatures from the age before consciousness still lived, hiding from a world that had forgotten them.

Of the thousands of **Elder Dragons** that had once ruled the skies in the era before consciousness, only **three** remained in all Oversee. They were the last survivors of the hunts of the **[[The Rapture|Rapture]]** — creatures who had chosen to withdraw from the world rather than become victims of civilization's endless ambition. They hid in places impossible for ordinary mortals to reach, waiting in long silence.

- **[[Vythrax]]** — the Silver Dragon, called **"The Silver Wind"** by the **[[The Frostscale Clan|Klan Frostscale]]**. She made her lair in the **[[The Spire of Whispering Ice|Spire of Whispering Ice]]**, a peak so high the air was too thin to breathe. From that height, she watched over her domain — including **[[Frosthaven Peak]]**, where Klan Frostscale built their settlement without fully realizing their deity was still alive, watching from a distance.
- **[[Verdantus]]** — the Green Dragon, guardian of the last primordial forest, buried beneath the canopy of the "Endless Canopy" in the Eastern Wilds. He was the guardian of flora's balance, ensuring the last ancient trees that filtered **Ambitio-Ether** were never cut down by greedy hands.
- **[[Aurelia]]** — the Blue Dragon, who had not surfaced in thousands of years. She swam at the bottom of the **Magic Lake** in the heart of Elis, managing the flow of mana in the water, ensuring the water sources of Elis remained pure and uncontaminated.

Meanwhile, the **[[The Behemoths|Behemoths]]** — giant mammals who had once walked across the world in thousands — now numbered only about a dozen. They roamed a hidden grassland surrounded by mountains in the heart of Elis — a plateau called by ancient explorers *"The Forgotten Garden."* Their deep footprints stabilized the soil, preventing erosion. They had no high consciousness, no magic calling the hunger from the sky. There was only an ancient rhythm: eat, sleep, breed, and unknowingly keep the lungs of the world breathing.

**Elis was a museum of the last life** — where the first act of the world was still staged in grand silence, without audience, without applause.

## The First Sin of Lei

**~45 AV**

But even the sacred silence of Elis was not immune to ambition creeping from the other continent.

In the forty-fifth year after the Valla era, the name **Klan Lei** had become synonymous with absolute power. **[[Lei Watigi]]** — the rightful heir of the water element from the **Lei Watigi** line — was the personification of that power. With **[[Safayer]]** — the blue soul-purifying stone pulsing in rhythm with his heart — he was a being almost perfect in the eyes of the world.

But perfection often breeds boredom, and boredom is a chasm waiting to be filled with arrogance.

In an expedition to the remote reaches of **Elis** — supposedly to map resources for the Commonwealth — Lei Watigi encountered something he had never seen in any scroll.

On the slopes of **Mount Frosthaven**, when he separated from his group to follow a more remote path, he crossed paths with **[[Vythrax]]**. The silver dragon was resting on an ice cliff, her scales reflecting the twilight sun into millions of cold sparks that blinded. For the first time in his life, Lei Watigi felt **small**. Vythrax was not merely a dragon. She was an ancient entity whose wisdom exceeded the age of the entire Lei bloodline.

But in Lei Watigi's eyes — accustomed to seeing the world as an object to be conquered — the splendor only sparked another desire: **the desire to possess something no one else could possess.**

He was not foolish enough to believe he could conquer a silver dragon with force. So he chose another path: **conspiracy.**

He approached the high priests of **[[The Frostscale Clan|Klan Frostscale]]** secretly — not with hostility, but with an offer they could not refuse. Great wealth. Influence in the Commonwealth. Access to the technology and resources of the Oversee continent. All he asked in return was one thing: **their help to have a child with their own deity.**

The priests — who for thousands of years had only been keepers of ritual without truly experiencing Vythrax's intimate presence — were tempted. They conspired in a scheme that would destroy the sanctity of their own clan.

## The Egg

The brief meeting between Lei Watigi and Vythrax — which initially seemed like chance — had actually been carefully arranged by the priests. They created a situation in which the silver dragon was in a vulnerable state — perhaps through a certain ritual that weakened her concentration, perhaps through a potion that made her more easily influenced. Lei Watigi returned again and again, bringing offerings of rare crystals said to strengthen the bond with the water element.

Vythrax — who had lived in solitude for thousands of years — initially regarded the small creature only as a nuisance. But Lei Watigi's words were full of flattery, and his eyes full of ambition spoke a language the dragon had never understood. With the charm of a noble who had never heard the word *no*, Lei Watigi **coerced** the silver dragon. A forbidden act that crossed the boundaries of race, instinct, and nature. And Vythrax — in a weakness she had never realized existed within herself — was **trapped**.

But the priests' conspiracy did not go smoothly. One of them — in a confession at a critical moment — leaked the foul plan to other clan members. News of the betrayal spread like fire across the ice field. The conspiring priests were tried by their own people, but it was all too late. **Vythrax — the deity they worshipped — had been seen to be pregnant.**

The dragon's womb — which for thousands of years had been only a vessel for pure ice heritage — now nurtured a foreign seed. Klan Frostscale was gripped by suffocating panic. The deity they worshipped — the protector they glorified with songs and offerings — was now carrying a mixed child of an arrogant noble from the other continent. This disgrace could not be known by anyone. The reputation of the clan, their relationship with Vythrax, the entire foundation of their trust — all threatened to collapse.

When Vythrax finally laid a large egg with a blue glow like ice frozen for thousands of years, the remaining elders — those not involved in the initial conspiracy but now having to clean up the mess — conspired in the greatest lie in the history of the clan.

They held a secret meeting in the temple's underground chamber, attended only by the highest priests still loyal. The egg, they decided to announce to the entire clan gathered in the temple, was not an ordinary egg. It was the **Breathstone** — a sacred heirloom, a direct gift from Vythrax, which would become a source of strength and eternal blessing for Klan Frostscale. They created a false worship ritual, built an intricately carved altar around it, and commanded the whole clan to bow every time the full moon came.

The egg was kept in the most sacred chamber in the heart of the temple, worshipped in silence for hundreds of years.

**Vythrax herself** — with a heart wounded but her oath of protection still held tightly — chose a lonely path. She could no longer remain among a clan that had built a lie on top of her disgrace. Before flying north to the **[[The Spire of Whispering Ice|Spire of Whispering Ice]]**, she infiltrated the temple one night, looking at her egg one last time.

In a sorrow beyond measure, she swallowed the **full Safayer** — the blue Gemheart, the purification stone, one of the four organs of the Architect. She had secretly taken it from Lei Watigi during their last meeting. She knew the blue stone had extraordinary purifying power. Perhaps by swallowing it, she could create a magical resonance that would protect her egg during her departure — an invisible shield that would keep the embryo within safe and slow its development until the right time. She did not want her child to be born in the midst of this chaos and lie. Better the egg waited, sleeping deeply, until the world was ready to receive it. Perhaps she also just wanted to carry something that reminded her of what had happened. Perhaps she wanted to punish herself.

## 700 AV — The Egg Hatches

**The Present Day**

Hundreds of years passed.

The blue egg continued to be kept and worshipped as the **Breathstone** — a sacred heirloom said to maintain the clan's harmony. Generation after generation of Frostscale priests passed, inheriting the same worship ritual without knowing the truth behind it. **Lei Watigi** had long since died, his ambition turned to dust. **Vythrax** continued to endure the Safayer poison flowing in her veins at the peak of the **Spire of Whispering Ice**, still faithfully watching from afar despite being unable to return to the home she had once protected.

Then, one day in the year **700 AV**, when the whole clan gathered in the full moon ritual, something that had not happened for centuries finally happened.

**The egg cracked.**

All eyes turned to the sacred altar as the blue shell shattered into pieces. From within it emerged a **baby boy** — not a dragon, not entirely human. His skin was pale as ice. His hair was silver-white. Small, faint horns grew at his temples. His newly opened eyes looked around blankly, not understanding why hundreds of eyes stared at him with a mixture of fear, confusion, and shock.

A suffocating silence enveloped the temple. The priests looked at each other in panic. The secret they had buried for centuries now stood naked before them in the form of a baby. The truth could no longer be hidden, but a new lie had to be created immediately.

They could not let anyone know that their sacred deity — **Vythrax, The Silver Wind** — had given birth to a mixed child of a human. That would destroy Vythrax's sanctity in the eyes of the clan. That would destroy the foundation of trust they had built.

So the priests quickly made an emergency decision. The baby would be kept secret. They announced that what had just happened was a miracle — that the **Breathstone** had "given birth" to a child destined to become the new guardian of the temple. But they could not let him stay in the sacred chamber. A Frostscale woman who had long desired a child but never had one was secretly summoned and given a sacred mandate: **raise this child in complete secrecy. Let no one know his origin. Let him not leave your house except at night. He belongs to the clan, but no one must know he exists.**

The woman — whose heart was too sincere to refuse the priests' mandate — took the baby home and raised him with love in hiding. She named him **[[Syl Frost]]** — a name that sounded like an ordinary ice child's, with no hint of his origin. During the early years, the secret was kept. Syl grew up in isolation, knowing only his adoptive mother and a few priests who occasionally came to check on him.

But blood never lies.

As he grew, Syl began to show strange, powerful magical abilities — something that a normal Frostscale child should not possess. He could control ice in ways never seen before, create snowstorms in closed rooms, and sometimes speak in ancient languages no one had taught him. In Frostheart society — where magic was viewed with suspicion and often considered taboo — Syl's talent became a major problem. Neighbors began to whisper. Rumors began to spread. His adoptive mother could no longer hide the reality that her adopted child was different.

The priests — who initially thought they could control the situation — began to panic. This secret could not last forever. If the truth about Syl's origin were exposed — that he was the child of Vythrax and Lei Watigi — then the entire lie about the sanctity of their deity would collapse. So they made a new decision: instead of protecting Syl, they would **ostracize** him. They began to spread rumors that Syl was a **bastard child**, the result of a forbidden relationship between his adoptive mother and a foreigner. They created a narrative that Syl's strange magic was proof of "dirty blood" flowing in his veins. That way, public attention would be diverted from the deeper truth: that he was actually the child of their own deity.

Syl grew up in that ostracism. He learned that he was different, that people looked at him with hatred and fear, that he would never be accepted as part of the clan. But he also began to feel something strange in his body — a weakness he could not explain. Sometimes he felt dizzy. Sometimes he saw blue flashes at the edge of his vision. Sometimes he felt something crawling in his blood. He did not know that it was the remnants of the **Safayer** poison Vythrax had swallowed centuries ago — the full stone, meant to protect him, but because he hatched hundreds of years later, his young, not-yet-fully-mature body could not overcome it. The magical resonance Vythrax created to protect her egg was now a curse gnawing at Syl from within.

**Vythrax** — from a distance at the peak of the Spire of Whispering Ice — felt that her child had finally been born. She felt the resonance of **Safayer** in Syl's blood, and her heart broke. Her attempt to protect had only hurt her child. But she was too weak, too poisoned by the blue stone, to come down and fetch him. She could only watch from afar, enduring the pain that continued to flow in her veins, hoping that one day her child would be strong enough to overcome the curse he had inherited.

And Syl — who knew nothing about all of this — continued to live in ostracism, carrying in his blood the poison of a forbidden love between an ambitious noble and a silver dragon — a love that should never have happened, but had created a child who would one day be one of the key figures in the journey of **[[oversee/pcs/README|The Five Sticks]]**.

## The Truth Beyond the Player-Facing Text

The player-facing text above is **the historical record as Klan Lei presents it** — sanitized, softened, and rewritten. It is what the party could piece together from the Cycle Clan's archives, the Edisi Law fragments, and the oral histories of the Keeper of Secret. It omits several truths.

### What the Party Does Not Know Yet

**Lei Firsa's status is ambiguous.**

The Cycle Clan records say "Lei the Great" ascended to godhood. The truth is that **Lei Firsa may or may not be alive**. She may have used the Gemhearts and forbidden rituals to extend her life for 700 years — or she may have died centuries ago, leaving her legacy (Klan Lei, the Triase, the Commonwealth) to carry on her work. She may be something between a lich and a god — or she may be a memory, a name that still pulls strings.

**The DM must decide before Season 3.** The choice shapes the final arc:
- **If she is alive** — she is the final boss. The party must find her, confront her, and decide what to do with her.
- **If she is dead** — her legacy is the final boss. The party must dismantle the system she built — the twin tradition, the Triase, the lie — and build something new.
- **If she is something else** — she is a mystery. The party must discover what she became, and whether she can be redeemed, destroyed, or understood.

**The four children were not conceived naturally.**

Lei Firsa was **already pregnant with quadruplets** when Simon was placed on the altar. She had conceived the children through magical means — using Simon's stolen essence, extracted before the ritual. The children are biologically Simon's, but manufactured. Simon does not know. He loved his children without knowing they were made from pieces of him.

**The twin-killing tradition was invented by Lei Firsa.**

It is not ancient. It is not a natural law. It is **policy** — created by Lei Firsa in the first century AV to concentrate magical power in the Lei bloodline. The first twins killed were not "unified" — they were **murdered** to eliminate competition. Every twin since has been murdered for the same reason.

**The Commonwealth was built to protect a lie.**

The Triase, the Garda Mawar, the Garda Besi — all of it exists to protect the secret that **Klan Lei's power depends on Simon remaining Valla**. If Simon is ever freed, the Lei bloodline loses its elemental power. The Commonwealth will do anything — *anything* — to prevent that.

**The Doctrine of Simon's Ascension is a fabrication.**

Simon did not ascend to godhood. He was **sacrificed**. He is not a god. He is a prisoner — a Halfling shepherd who has been holding back cosmic hunger for 700 years, in constant pain, unable to move, unable to speak, unable to die.

The party will meet him in Season 3. He will not be a god. He will be a small, weeping Halfling in a cloak too large for him. His feet are bare. He has been standing for 700 years.

**Lei Watigi's assault on Vythrax was not seduction.**

The player-facing text says Lei Watigi "coerced" Vythrax. The truth is worse.

**Lei Watigi raped Vythrax.** The priests of Klan Frostscale helped — they weakened Vythrax with rituals and potions, making her vulnerable. Lei Watigi used that vulnerability to force himself on the dragon. When Vythrax became pregnant, the priests panicked — not because of what had been done to their deity, but because of the **disgrace** it would bring to the clan.

The "conspiracy" the player-facing text mentions was not about the assault — it was about **covering up the assault**. The priests who "leaked the plan" were not trying to save Vythrax — they were trying to expose Lei Watigi and save themselves.

**Syl is the product of rape.**

This is the darkest truth in his backstory. His mother was assaulted. His father — Lei Watigi — was a monster. The "forbidden love" in the player-facing text is a lie. There was no love. There was only violation.

**The Safayer in Syl's blood is killing him.**

Vythrax swallowed the **full Safayer** — the entire blue Gemheart — to protect her egg. The stone was meant to slow the egg's development and shield it from harm. But Syl hatched **hundreds of years later** — much later than Vythrax anticipated. The Safayer had been poisoning Vythrax for centuries, and the residual poison in Syl's blood is now slowly killing him.

**Syl's blood is also a universal cure.** The Safayer's purifying properties have made his blood an antidote to any poison, disease, or curse. This is a gift — but it is also a death sentence.

**The Behemoth's Tear can save him.** A single tear from the last Behemoth can heal any wound — including the poison in Syl's blood. The party will need to find the Behemoth in Season 3.

### The Major Contradiction — Resolved

**How did Lei Firsa have four children if Simon was on the altar? — RESOLVED**

Lei Firsa was **already pregnant with quadruplets** when Simon was placed on the altar. She had conceived the children through magical means before the ritual — using Simon's stolen essence. The children are biologically Simon's, but manufactured. Simon does not know. He loved his children without knowing they were made from pieces of him.

**This is the canon.** No further resolution needed.
