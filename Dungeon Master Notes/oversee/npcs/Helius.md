- Found Shin as a child on the shore of Elis. Took him in without asking questions.

### Who Helius Is
- **Helius** was a **simple man** — a fisherman, a farmer, a hermit. He lived alone on the shore of **Elis**, in a small hut near the water. He did not want much. He did not need much. He had his faith, his bread, and his silence.
- He was a **Valla worshipper** — not in the way the priests worshipped, with rituals and temples and gold, but in the simplest way. He prayed before sleep. He shared his bread with strangers. He sat in silence and listened to the wind. He believed in something greater than himself — not because he had proof, but because he had **hope**.
- He was **not a hero**. He was not a warrior. He was not a mage. He was a man who had lived a long life and had seen too much. He had been an adventurer once — a long time ago — but he had retired after his party died. He had lost everyone he loved. He had chosen solitude. He had chosen peace.
- **He found Shin on the shore.** A child, alone, wearing gloves, smiling a smile that did not reach his eyes. He did not ask where he came from. He did not ask who his parents were. He did not ask about the gloves. He simply said: *"You look like you need a warm meal."*

### Helius and Shin
- Helius **raised Shin** as his own. He did not adopt him formally — there were no papers, no ceremonies, no legal records. He simply took him in. He fed him. He clothed him. He taught him to read. He taught him to listen. He taught him to sit in silence without needing to fill it.
- **He never asked Shin to remove his gloves.** He never asked about the curse mark. He never asked about the smile. He did not care. He saw a child who needed help. He helped.
- **He taught Shin to be human.** He taught him to fish. He taught him to cook. He taught him to mend nets and fix roofs and chop wood. He taught him that the world was not just monsters and darkness — it was also sunlight and bread and the sound of waves.
- **He taught Shin to believe in something.** Not in the way the priests believed — with fear and trembling — but in the way a father believes in his son. He taught him that Valla was not a god to be worshipped, but a promise to be kept. He taught him that the world was worth saving. He taught him that **he** was worth saving.
- **He sent Shin to The Grace** when he was grown. He told him: *"You need a purpose. And the world needs someone like you."* He did not know what Shin would face. He did not know what Shin would become. He only knew that Shin needed to leave. He needed to find his own path. He needed to be more than a fisherman's son.

### What Helius Knew
- **Helius was a former adventurer.** He had been part of a party once — a long time ago. They had fought demons. They had fought Gap-touched creatures. They had fought things that should not exist. They had died. He had survived. He had retired. He had chosen solitude.
- **He had seen HVS shells in his adventuring days.** He had seen the tubes. He had seen the empty bodies. He had seen the Cycle Clan's work. He knew what they were. He knew what they were for. He knew they were designed to hold something — something old, something powerful, something that should not be contained in a mortal body.
- **He knew what Shin was.** He knew the child he found on the shore was not a normal child. He knew the gloves, the smile, the silence — they were not quirks. They were **signs**. They were the marks of a shell. They were the marks of something that had been designed to hold a god.
- **He never told Shin.** He never told him he was HVS-02. He never told him he was a shell. He never told him he was meant to hold Zargoth. He wanted Shin to be human. He wanted Shin to have a childhood. He wanted Shin to have a choice.
- **His last words to Shin** — whispered on his deathbed — were: *"You are not a shell. You are my son. Whatever else you are, that is true."*
- **Shin has never understood what he meant.** He thinks it was just a dying man's rambling. He does not know Helius knew the truth. He does not know Helius was trying to tell him. He does not know Helius was trying to set him free.

### Helius's Death
- Helius died of **old age**. He died in his sleep. He died in his hut on the shore of Elis. He died with a smile on his face — a real smile, not a curse, not a mask. A smile of peace.
- **Shin was not there.** He had already left for The Grace. He had already begun his training. He had already begun his life. He did not know Helius was dying. He did not know he would never see him again.
- **Shin learned of Helius's death months later.** A letter from a neighbor. A simple note: *"Your father is gone. He died in his sleep. He was smiling."*
- **Shin broke.** He wept. He screamed. He cursed the gods he did not believe in. He cursed himself for leaving. He cursed the world for taking the only person who had ever loved him.
- **He has never fully recovered.** He carries the grief with him. He carries it in his silence. He carries it in his prayers. He carries it in the way he treats the people he loves — as if they might disappear at any moment.

### Helius's Grave
- **Helius's grave** is still in Elis, near the shore where he found Shin. It is a simple grave — a wooden marker, a pile of stones, a small patch of wildflowers. No one has visited it in 690 years.
- **The grave is still there.** The wooden marker has rotted. The stones have scattered. The wildflowers have grown wild. But the grave is still there — a small, quiet place where a simple man was laid to rest.
- **If Shin ever visits the grave**, he will break. He will weep. He will kneel. He will say: *"I am sorry I left. I am sorry I was not there. I am sorry I did not say goodbye."* He will stay for hours. He will not speak. He will simply sit in silence — the silence Helius taught him to love.
- **The grave is a Season 3 optional scene.** It is not essential to the plot. It is essential to Shin's arc. It is the moment when he finally grieves. It is the moment when he finally lets go.
